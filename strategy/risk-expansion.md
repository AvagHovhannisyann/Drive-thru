# CHAMPA: risk register and expansion strategy

*Strategy workstream "Risk & expansion". Prepared 2026-10-10. Currency: AMD. USD at the CBA rate of **AMD 361.93/USD** (9 Oct 2026; A1 [1], VERIFIED).*

**How to read the evidence tags.** The status tags follow the research files: **VERIFIED**, **REPORTED**, **ESTIMATE** (the formula is shown) and **ASSUMPTION** (the basis is stated). Each citation gives the research file and that file's own source number. For example, "B1 [1]" is source 1 in `research/B1-m4-traffic.md`, and "A2 [S9]" uses A2's S-numbering. Where a research file's fact-check corrected a value, this document uses the corrected value and marks it **FC**. Two figures come from outside the ten assigned files, and both were read directly: labour data from C3-labor.md and fiscal-device rules from C4-tax.md. Nothing below comes from memory or web search.

> **Scope and scoring basis.** Each risk is scored twice.
> - **P (prototype as specified):** the founder's concept, a standalone premium drive-thru on a roadside M4 plot between Abovyan and Sevan. It uses the 171 m² building on a 3,256 m² plot with new decel/accel lanes and right-in/right-out access.
> - **R (recommended configuration):** the configuration after mitigation. That means a year-round Yerevan exit-corridor hybrid. An M4 unit is acceptable only at the Yerevan end, with interchange, local-road or forecourt access. Capex is value-engineered and expansion is stage-gated.
>
> The scores are analyst judgement (ASSUMPTION) on the scales in §1.1. They are meant to rank and compare risks, not to feed the model.

## 0. Bottom line

1. **Five risks score "red" for the prototype as specified. Most of them come from three choices: site type, capex scale and throughput design.**
   - The five red risks are capex over-specification, demand shortfall, seasonality, highway-access permitting and queue spill-back onto a 90 km/h road.
   - Moving to a year-round commuter site with secondary-road access and value-engineered capex moves most of them to amber. This is the "R" column in the register.
2. **The prototype is flagship-class in cost, and the evidence does not support that capex for a first unit.**
   - C1's comparable flagship (180 m² on 3,500 m², 2 lanes) costs **AMD 376.3M ($1.04M)**. That is VAT-inclusive and excludes land, kitchen/coffee equipment and pre-opening.
   - At C6's reference unit (150 tx/day × AMD 2,600 × 360 days = AMD 140.4M of sales, at a 15–20% four-wall margin), simple payback is **13–18 years** (ESTIMATE, §2.2).
   - A ≤4-year payback at that capex needs about **270–280 tx/day**. C6 calls that order of volume implausible for Armenia, and A2 puts a well-sited unit at 80–150 tx/day.
3. **The unit sits close to a profit cliff.**
   - Contribution after COGS, card fees and turnover tax is about 60% of sales. Breakeven is therefore about **100–113 tx/day**, consistent with C6's "near breakeven below ≈100 tx/day".
   - A 10% volume miss removes **30–40% of four-wall EBITDA** (ESTIMATE, R01).
   - Demand evidence is therefore the gating item for everything else. No M4 traffic count has been published since 2008 (B1 [1]).
4. **The competitive window is real but short.**
   - Coffee House already runs the only drive-thru in Armenia, at M4 km ≈12.8, at roughly half our price point. Its weakness is operational: written reviews average 3.76★, with 20–60-minute waits (A3 [22]).
   - Fuel retailers are rolling out cafés: Max Cafe on about 11 stations including both M4 carriageways at km 12.2 (FC), and Flash Express Cafe "opening soon" (A3 [24][25]).
   - Starbucks via Alshaya (talks in Oct 2025; Georgia launch reported in Jul 2026) and Wendy's (20 units planned by 2030) are 1–4-year threats. They hit "American-style" positioning more than an Armenian-rooted brand such as CHAMPA (A2 [S5]–[S7]; A3 [11][12]).
5. **One highway unit cannot seed a chain on its own** (§2.1).
   - Its customers are low-frequency and seasonal, and its local labour pool is thin.
   - Its results would not transfer to the Yerevan arterial sites where a chain's density has to come from.
   - In the region, drive-thru scaled as **urban-arterial QSR** (all 21 tagged Georgian drive-thrus are urban). Roadside coffee scaled through **fuel forecourts** (WOG, OKKO, Dunkin' at Wissol) (A5 [32][19][22]).
6. **Recommended expansion model: a Yerevan hub-and-spoke cluster.**
   - Unit 1 is a proof-and-training store on a year-round exit corridor (B4's top five).
   - Unit 2 is on a second corridor within 30–40 minutes of Unit 1.
   - A **forecourt pilot** with a fuel retailer that has no food concept of its own (CPS or an independent CNG station) gives low-capex coverage of the M4, M-5, M-2 and M-1. C1 estimates a co-located lean unit at **AMD 70.5M**.
   - The Sevan and Tsaghkadzor leisure peaks are served by **one relocatable pod**, not a fixed asset.
   - Gyumri and Vanadzor come last, as walk-up or partner units.
7. **No second location without passing Gate G1** (§2.7). G1 requires, over trading months 7–12 and including at least two trough months:
   - **≥1.35× the unit's own measured breakeven volume, seasonally adjusted** (≈140–150 tx/day at the C6 reference cost base), with no month below breakeven;
   - **four-wall EBITDA ≥15%** and an annualised payback run-rate of ≤4 years;
   - **median order-to-handoff ≤3:00**, demonstrated window capacity of ≥60 cars/h and **zero queue spill-back events**;
   - order accuracy ≥95% and a written-review average of ≥4.3★;
   - crew turnover ≤80% a year, two transferable shift leads, and a GM who is not the founder;
   - a registered HACCP plan;
   - a **written permit playbook**, with comfort letters for the Unit 2 site before any land commitment.
8. **Capital should be released in tranches tied to gates.**
   - Founder or angel equity funds validation and Unit 1.
   - Asset-backed debt or leasing, plus partner-funded forecourt pads, fund Units 2–3 after G1.
   - Growth or strategic equity, development-finance SME lines and regional franchising come only after G2.
   - Buying land would add **AMD 78–177M** for a 3,256 m² Kotayk plot, or **AMD 189–412M** at the Yerevan periphery (ESTIMATE from B4 [16][17] asking prices). A ground lease or partner pad is strongly preferred.

#### R01: Demand shortfall (traffic over-estimated and/or low conversion)
**P 4 × 5 = 20 · R 3 × 4 = 12 · Owner: Strategy lead**

**Evidence**

*Traffic*
- No M4 count has been published since 2008. The last was 21,505 vehicles/day at km 10+320 (B1 [1], VERIFIED).
- Every current M4 volume is an extrapolation (B1 §3.4, ESTIMATE): Yerevan edge 33–54k AADT (base 42k); Abovyan–Charentsavan 18.2–40.5k (base 27.3k); Hrazdan–Sevan 9.9–27k (base 16.8k).
- B1 itself calls data risk "the largest risk".
- B3's bottom-up leisure sizing (0.15–0.75M car round trips a year) equals only **6–24%** of B1's Hrazdan–Sevan base AADT. Either the AADT estimate is high, or the leisure share is low (B3 §3.8, ESTIMATE).

*Capture*
- The model's base input (1.8% × 0.55 accessible share) equals **≈2.4% of same-side light vehicles**. That is above the US-mature central value.
- C6 recommends a mature capture of **0.3–1.3% on the M4 (base ≈0.7%)** and **0.5–1.1% on urban edges** (C6 §3.6, ESTIMATE).
- At B1's ~11,000 same-side light vehicles on Abovyan–Charentsavan, 0.75% capture gives **83 tx/day**. The model input would give ≈290/day (C6 §3.6).

*Profit cliff*
- Contribution ratio ≈ 60% of sales (ESTIMATE): 100% − 33% COGS incl. waste (C6 §3.3) − ≈1.5% card fees (A1 §3.9: 1.5–2% uncapped, on a 75–90% card share; FC: the fee cap does not apply above AMD 150M turnover a year) − ≈5.5% effective catering turnover tax (C4 #44: 5.2–6.25%).
- On C6's reference unit (AMD 140.4M of sales; four-wall EBITDA of AMD 21–28M), fixed costs ≈ AMD 56–63M a year.
- Breakeven ≈ fixed ÷ (0.60 × 2,600 × 360) ≈ **100–113 tx/day** (ESTIMATE). C6 independently says a unit is "near breakeven" below ≈100 tx/day.
- A 10% volume miss costs ≈ 0.1 × 140.4 × 0.60 = AMD 8.4M, i.e. **30–40% of four-wall EBITDA** (ESTIMATE).

*Format odds*
- A5 rates a standalone premium drive-only M4 unit at **≈25–35%** probability of hitting base-case economics. A drive-thru-led hybrid on a Yerevan exit arterial is rated ≈50–60% (A5 §4, ASSUMPTION).
- Armenia has **0** OSM-tagged drive-thrus, against 21 in Georgia, all of them urban (A5 [32]).

*Disconfirming evidence:* the incumbent's peak queues prove that demand exists (A3 [22]).

**EWI**
- Before commitment, any of these is a warning:
  - a 7-day classified count at the plot showing **<17,300 same-side light vehicles/day** at an urban edge, or **<18,600** on a highway (ESTIMATE: 130 lane tx/day ÷ base capture of 0.75% or 0.70%, C6 §3.6);
  - an observed stop rate at comparable outlets below 0.5%;
  - Coffee House's M4 drive-thru below ≈100 cars/day off-season.
- After opening, any of these is a warning:
  - tx/day below 85% of plan for 4 consecutive weeks after month 3;
  - seasonally adjusted tx/day below 1.1× breakeven at month 6.

**Mitigation**
1. Commit no land before the B1 §5 fieldwork.
   - Lean pre-screen: **US$4–8k per parcel**.
   - Full two-season programme: **US$18–35k ≈ AMD 6.5–12.7M** (B1 §5.2 ASSUMPTION; ESTIMATE at 361.93).
   - In parallel, request RDF/MTAI counter data and Road Police camera counts (B1 §5.1).
2. Observe the Coffee House M4 drive-thru: 3 weekdays + 2 weekend days × 3 dayparts, in summer and winter. C6 calls this "the single most decisive calibration point".
3. Re-base the model's capture to **0.30 / 0.60 / 0.90%** on the model basis, and keep 1.8% as a stretch case only (C6 §4.1).
4. Shortlist only sites that need ≤0.75% capture for 120 tx/day. B4's urban edges need 0.49–0.57%; the M4 Hrazdan–Sevan segment needs 1.72% (B4 §3.7).
5. Keep the hybrid design: walk-up window, the 12 seats and terrace, and hot food. This widens the funnel beyond the 41.2% of households that own a car (A1 [9]).
6. Commission a consumer survey (n ≥ 600; Yerevan and Kotayk; quotas by car ownership; Van Westendorp price ladder), as specified in A5 §5 and A1 §5.

#### R02: Seasonality and weekend concentration
**P 5 × 4 = 20 · R 3 × 2 = 6 · Owner: Strategy and Ops**

**Evidence**
- B3's corridor index (base case, mean = 100; ESTIMATE from Armstat [1][3][4][5]):

  | Segment | Jan | Feb | Aug | Peak/trough | Jun–Aug share of year |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Yerevan edge–Charentsavan | 86 | 84 | 125 | 1.50 | 29.3% |
  | Hrazdan–Sevan | 69 | 66 | 151 | 2.28 | 34.0% |
  | Sevan–Dilijan | — | — | — | 2.56 | — |

- B4 proposes an urban index of 0.77–1.15 (B4 §3.8, ASSUMPTION).
- Lake Sevan coastal water reaches ≥20 °C only in Jul–Aug (21.2 °C in both months of 2026; B3 [6], VERIFIED). The leisure peak sits on roughly 10–14 summer weekends, with peak days at 2.0–2.4× AADT (B3 §3.7, ASSUMPTION).
- Gegharkunik earns only **1.8%** of national hotel revenue (B3 [7], VERIFIED).
- Analog: McDonald's seasonal outlet in Budva closed for "lack of interest for a permanent location" (A5 [14], REPORTED).

**EWI**
- Jan–Feb revenue below 70% of the trailing monthly average.
- Jun–Aug above 34% of annual revenue.
- Summer weekend/weekday tx ratio above 1.8.
- Paid idle crew hours in trough months above 25% of the roster.

**Mitigation**
1. Put Unit 1 where year-round commuter demand alone covers fixed costs, as A5 §4.7 requires: a segment-A or urban-edge site (B3 §4.3; B4 §4.2).
2. Make the trough month at or above breakeven an explicit gate condition (G0.1, G1.1). At Hrazdan–Sevan's trough index of 0.66–0.69, keeping 100 tx/day in the trough needs an annual average of ≈145–152 tx/day (ESTIMATE: 100 ÷ 0.69 and 100 ÷ 0.66).
3. Serve the Sevan and Tsaghkadzor peaks with a **relocatable pod** (Format D, §2.3), not a fixed asset.
4. Flex rosters by season (C3 builds the roster bottom-up) and run weekday commuter loyalty and breakfast bundles.
5. Model with segment-specific indices and stress-test B-high and C-high (B3 §3.7).

#### R03: Incumbent response (Coffee House price war, fuel-station cafés)
**P 4 × 4 = 16 · R 4 × 3 = 12 · Owner: CEO and Marketing**

**Evidence**

*The Coffee House drive-thru*
- It is the only drive-thru in Armenia: M4 km ≈12.8, Sevan-bound side, open 08:30–24:00 (Yandex showed "until 23:00"; FC).
- Ratings: 4.9★ on 123 ratings. Prices: cappuccino 800–1,200, iced drinks 900 (A2 [S9][S10]; A3 [21][22]).
- Its weakness is operational. The 17 written reviews average **3.76★**, and **4 of the 5 written in Jun–Sep 2026 are 1–2★**, for 20–60-minute waits and wrong orders (A3 [22]).
- A3's verdict: "a fix is cheap for Coffee House".

*Coffee House as a company*
- 57 branches claimed and 52 listed (41 in Yerevan city plus the airport; FC); 250+ staff.
- App pre-ordering already works.
- FEIN premium sub-brand with an in-house Probat roaster since Sept 2025 (A2 [S8][S26]; A3 §3.7).

*Price gap*
- CHAMPA's affordable-premium band (cappuccino 1,400–1,600) sits **40–60% above** the mass leader (A2 §3.6, ESTIMATE/ASSUMPTION).

*Fuel retail*
- Max Cafe is on 10 tagged Max Oil stations, about 11 including km 12.2 left (FC), with both M4 carriageways at km 12.2.
- Flash Express Cafe is "opening soon", and Flash already has an Express Shop in Tsaghkadzor.
- Shell Café is present (FC) (A3 [24][25]).
- A3's speed estimates: Coffee House and Max Oil can respond within months; Flash within 6–12 months.
- The coffee-plus-hot-food white space is "time-limited" (A3 §4.5).

**EWI**
- No new Coffee House drive-thru reviews mention waits for 3 consecutive months, i.e. they have fixed operations.
- Coffee House announces a second drive-thru, or runs promotions of ≥15% within 5 km of our site.
- maxoil.am or flashpetrol.am news of a pickup-window café on our corridor.
- Our tx/day falls ≥10% within 6 weeks of any such event.

**Mitigation**
1. Stay out of the km 10–13 cluster (A3 §4.2).
   - On the M4, prefer the side the incumbent cannot serve. Morning commuter coffee runs on the **Yerevan-bound** carriageway; the incumbent sits on the Sevan-bound side (B1 §4.4; A3 §3.3).
2. Compete on what is slow and expensive to copy, not on base price:
   - a published service-time promise and lane timers;
   - a narrow hot savoury menu (A3 §4.5; A2 §4.6);
   - friendly service ("Ճամփադ բաց!"). Intouch found friendly visits were also 25 s faster (C6 §3.2.3).
3. Keep an **entry tier of ≤AMD 1,200 coffee** so we are not a special-occasion stop (A3 §4.4; A5 §4.4 puts base coffee at 900–1,300).
4. Pre-agree a price-response rule (ASSUMPTION):
   - never match mass price on core espresso;
   - respond with bundles and loyalty credit;
   - hold a contribution floor per transaction.
5. Move quickly on the white-space corridors. At the M-2 south edge there are 0 cafés within 1 km and the nearest Coffee House is 4.6 km away (B4 [10][19]).
6. Monitor competitor prices and openings monthly (A3 §5).

#### R04: International entrants (Starbucks/Alshaya; Wendy's and KFC drive lanes)
**P 3 × 2 = 6 · R 3 × 3 = 9 · Owner: CEO**

**Evidence**

*Starbucks*
- Starbucks-Alshaya Group Eurasia held exploratory talks with the Economy Minister on 8 Oct 2025 (A2 [S5][S6], REPORTED).
- Alshaya's first Georgian store (Tbilisi) was reported on 17 Jul 2026 (A2 [S7], REPORTED; the A2 fact-check confirmed the headline, while A3 could not verify it).
- A2's view is a 12–36-month risk, most likely starting in malls and the city centre (ASSUMPTION).
- Starbucks' FY2025 10-K **dropped "particularly drive-thru formats"** in favour of "a seat for every occasion". Its entry would therefore be coffeehouse-first (C6 [7][8], VERIFIED).

*Wendy's and KFC*
- Wendy's opened in Yerevan in Dec 2025 (A3 [11], VERIFIED) and plans 20 restaurants by 2030 (A3 [12], REPORTED).
- Wendy's runs 7 of Georgia's 21 tagged drive-thrus (A5 [32]).
- KFC's Homplex unit in Proshyan is its most plausible lane site (A3, FC).

*Track record*
- Stars Coffee announced Armenia in 2022 and never opened (A2 [S27]).

**EWI**
- Alshaya Armenia job ads or mall lease news.
- Starbucks trademark activity in Armenia.
- Growth in Georgian store count.
- Wendy's freestanding-site permits or suburban job ads.
- Construction of a drive lane at KFC Homplex.

**Mitigation**
1. Keep CHAMPA's Armenian identity: "American service standards, not American identity" (A5 §4.8). Do not market "first American-style drive-thru". It is factually wrong on the M4 (A3 §4.1) and invites a direct comparison with the original.
2. Own the drive-thru niche that Starbucks is de-emphasising (C6 §4.8). Build loyalty density before entry: target ≥10% of transactions identified via app or loyalty by month 12 (G1.6).
3. Secure 1–2 suburban pads early, as A3 §4.6 suggests, but through gates. Use options with conditions precedent rather than purchases.

#### R05: Highway-access permit failure or delay
**P 4 × 5 = 20 · R 3 × 3 = 9 · Owner: Development lead**

**Evidence**

*Lane requirements (ՀՀՇՆ 32-01-2022, C5 [5], VERIFIED)*
- Any connection to an IA–IC road with ≥50 PCU/day in or out needs **acceleration lanes of 140–230 m plus an 80 m taper**, and **deceleration lanes of 90–110 m plus an 80 m taper**.
- That is ≈**1,155–1,500 m²** of heavy-duty pavement, costing **AMD 27–43M ($75–119k)** (C5 §3.5, ESTIMATE).
- C1 carried only 700 m² / AMD 17.8M (mid) and 1,125 m² / AMD 28M (flagship).

*Approval process*
- The road owner (MTAI) and the Police must agree.
- **No statutory deadline or fee schedule exists** (C5 [1] Art. 13(16), 15.3).
- A road-safety audit has applied to interstate roads since 1 Jan 2026. Whether it covers a private junction is unclear (C5 [1] Art. 19.2).

*Road category*
- An IB expressway allows right-in/right-out only. A new median opening for a private business is "very unlikely" (C5 §3.5, ASSUMPTION).
- 90 km/h applies on about 93% of the dual carriageway (B1 [23]).
- US kiosk franchisor Scooter's rejects roads above 45 mph (72 km/h) (C6 [23]).

*Precedent*
- The Coffee House drive-thru shows the format can be permitted.
- The first 9.8 km of the M4 already has an approved development scheme (C5 [3] GD 2404-N, Annex 3).

**EWI**
- No written technical conditions within 60 days of the MTAI pre-application.
- The road category is confirmed as IB.
- A road-safety audit is demanded.
- The required lane area exceeds 1,300 m².
- The Police object to the traffic scheme.

**Mitigation**
1. Prefer sites accessed from a **secondary road or an existing interchange**. GD 2404-N §5(ա) steers service access there, and it removes speed-change lanes entirely (C5 §4.1).
   - On the M4, the Nurnus service areas at km 21.5 already have **slip ramps on both sides** (B1 §3.8 #10). They are a candidate for a forecourt unit.
2. Pull Coffee House's Ptghni permit trail (cadastre and community records) to learn what MTAI accepts (C5 §4.9).
3. Land contracts should be **options with conditions precedent** that require written MTAI/Police access agreement.
4. If a roadside plot is unavoidable, budget AMD 27–43M, not C1's 17.8M, and assume the uphill case (230 m acceleration lane) on the Abovyan–Charentsavan climb (C5 §3.5; B1 §3.7).
5. Hire a permitting agent with recent Kotayk files (C5 §5).

#### R06: Land designation and master-plan delays
**P 4 × 4 = 16 · R 3 × 3 = 9 · Owner: Development lead**

**Evidence**

*Process without a master plan (GD 1918-N, C5 [9], VERIFIED for the steps)*
- The route is community → marzpet → regional commission → MTAI → interagency commission, then the council.
- The steps total ≈28 working days plus a council session; C5's realistic estimate is 2–4 months.
- The interagency commission must be **unanimous**. Otherwise the case goes to a Government committee.

*Cost and overlays*
- The owner pays the **cadastral-value difference** (C5 [8] Land Code Art. 7(8)). The amount for any given site was not found.
- Interstate-road special-regulation coordination adds community 3 + marzpetaran 5 + Urban Development Committee 15 working days (C5 [4] GD 792-N).

#### R19: Partner dependence (fuel-station host, landlord)
**P 2 × 3 = 6 · R 3 × 3 = 9 · Owner: Development lead**

**Evidence**

*Precedents*
- In Georgia, 27 of 72 mapped Dunkin' outlets sit within 60 m of a fuel station, 20 of them Wissol. The operator relationship is unverified (A5 [32]).
- WOG's coffee business reached ≈117–181 cups per point per day (C6 [28] and A5 [19], ESTIMATES).

*Partner landscape (A3 [24][25][26])*

- **CPS shows no food concept** and claims 101 stations (53 listed). Its stations include Verin Ptghni, Abovyan, Hrazdan, Sevan, Varser and Dilijan.
- 36 of 60 M4 stations are unbranded independents.

*Classification risk*
- A café inside a fuel-station complex could be classed **category IV** (state expertise), and fuel storage of ≥5 m³ is EIA Category B (C5 [7][26]).

**EWI**
- The partner launches its own café.
- The partner seeks renegotiation within the first 24 months.
- The partner station's fuel volume declines.
- The community or Urban Development Committee classifies the unit as part of the station.

**Mitigation**
1. Sign long-term leases (≥10 years, ASSUMPTION) with exclusivity on coffee and hot food, step-in rights and pre-agreed relocation compensation.
2. Prefer a **separately titled adjacent plot** that shares the access, to keep the unit in category II. Get written classification first (C5 §4.2).
3. Structure the deal as fixed rent plus a capped revenue share. A3 §4.3 says to model both; terms are a gap.
4. Use relocatable modular buildings, whose relocation option C1 values (§4.3).

#### R20: Financing and liquidity
**P 4 × 4 = 16 · R 3 × 3 = 9 · Owner: CFO**

**Evidence**

**EWI**

- Unit 1 needs shareholder top-ups after month 6.

- The opening slips more than 3 months.

**Mitigation**

2. Fund Unit 1 with equity only.
3. Use ground leases or partner pads rather than land purchase.
4. Lease equipment where possible.
5. Commit no Unit 2 construction until funding is fully closed (G1.12).

### 2.9 What would change this recommendation

**Towards the M4 sooner**
- Field counts at the Coffee House drive-thru and at an M4 Yerevan-end parcel show ≥150 cars/day **in winter**.
- MTAI confirms that a local-road or interchange access is acceptable.
- CPS (or an independent at the Nurnus slip-ramp service areas) offers a pad on a revenue share that clears G0.5.

**Towards a bigger first unit**
- Counted volume supports ≥180–195 tx/day (§2.2).
- The Ministry of Environment rules that site paving does not trigger an EIA.
- Contractor quotes come in at least 25% below C1.

**Against expansion altogether**
- The survey shows weak willingness to pay at 1,400–1,600 AMD.
- Coffee House fixes its drive-thru operations and opens a second drive-thru before our Unit 1.
- A Russia–Armenia rupture pushes AMD/USD above 390 (A1 stress bound) together with a slump in hospitality turnover.
