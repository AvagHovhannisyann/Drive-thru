Phase 1 · Market research

The Armenian market: demand, competition and drive-thru fit

People in Armenia do buy coffee from the car, but the opening the founder assumed is already taken: Coffee House has run a drive-thru on the M4 at about km 12.8 since late 2024, selling coffee at roughly 700–1,200 AMD ($1.93–3.32) on a menu Yandex flags as stale REPORTED [A2 §2 #16], and Max Oil runs walk-in cafés on both carriageways at km 12.2. The evidence supports a narrower opening (faster, more accurate service and hot Armenian hand-held food at affordable-premium prices, on a year-round commuter corridor near Yerevan or a fuel forecourt) and contradicts a premium, American-branded unit on the open highway. Even the narrower opening is unproven: the 80–150 transactions a day the evidence supports sit at or below the model's EBITDA break-even of about 150 a day, and that break-even already assumes a 2,691 AMD ticket against the 1,540–1,860 AMD that local prices suggest [MODEL; A4 §3.11].

1. Who can buy: incomes, cars and payments

Incomes are low against an American ticket. The average gross wage was 312,486 AMD ($863) a month in Jan–Aug 2026 VERIFIED [A1 §2]. Kotayk and Gegharkunik, the M4 marzes, pay 58% and 44% of Yerevan's 357,583 AMD ($988) VERIFIED [A1 §3.3]. A 2,000 AMD drink costs about 1.5 hours of net work for an average earner ESTIMATE [A1 §1 #5]. Urban residents report spending 2,293 AMD ($6.34) a month on all food away from home, 3.5% of consumption VERIFIED. National catering turnover implies about 8,400 AMD per resident per month, about five times the national survey figure of 1,709 AMD ESTIMATE, so the survey misses tourists, business spending and high earners [A1 §3.6]. Poverty is 16.9% in Yerevan, 24.8% in Kotayk and 27.6% in Gegharkunik VERIFIED, though Kotayk's confidence interval (13.9–35.8%) is too wide to call the gap robust [A1 §7 #20].

Demand sits in Yerevan, and car access is modest. Yerevan holds 37.0% of residents VERIFIED [A1 §2]; the M4 corridor towns hold 167,262 VERIFIED [B3 §2 #27]. A highway unit therefore depends on Yerevan-origin leisure trips and on corridor-town commuters driving into Yerevan [A1 §1 #7; B3 §1 #8]. 41.2% of households own a car VERIFIED [A1 §7 #19]; the Caucasus Barometer gives 46% nationally and 42% in Yerevan VERIFIED [A5 §2 #3]. Taxis carried 28.1% of commercial-transport passengers in 2024 VERIFIED. The figure excludes private cars and walking (A1 fact-check correction), so it shows the weight of ride-hailing in paid transport and cannot be read as a car-free share [A1 §7 #22]. Car-free customers need a walk-up window.

Prices, FX and payments. CPI was 4.3% y/y in September 2026 and food 6.2% REPORTED [A1 §3.4]; the retail price of coffee rose 14.7% in 2025 VERIFIED [A1 §3.4]; AI-95 is at a record 630 AMD/l ($1.74) REPORTED, a drag on leisure driving [A1 §3.8]. The dram is at 361.93 per USD VERIFIED, near its 12-month strongest level; A1 recommends stress-testing 345–390 ASSUMPTION [A1 §3.5]. Of 5.47 m cards VERIFIED, about 86.9% are NFC-enabled ESTIMATE, and the average card purchase at a terminal fell from 6,654 AMD (Q1 2024) to 3,473 AMD ($9.60) in Q2 2026 ESTIMATE. Cards are now used for small everyday baskets (A1 matches the ticket to a 1,500–4,000 AMD coffee-and-snack purchase), though cash withdrawals were still about 44% of card turnover in H1 2026 ESTIMATE [A1 §1 #8; A1 §3.9]. Apple Pay works with 17 issuers VERIFIED [A1 §3.9].

The card-fee cap. The CBA's acquiring-fee cap (0.5% ArCa, 0.9% Visa/Mastercard) applies only up to 150 M AMD ($414k) of annual turnover REPORTED, about 411k AMD a day [A1 §7 #15]. At the volumes the evidence supports, a unit would stay under it: even 150 orders at 1,860 AMD is about 279k AMD a day ESTIMATE. The model's conservative case turns over 50–84 M AMD ($139–233k) a year, also under the cap; only its base case passes it, from year 2 (186.5 M AMD, $515k) [MODEL]. A1's advice to use the uncapped 1.5–2% fee assumed a 3,000 AMD ticket. Keeping that fee in the base case is the conservative choice ASSUMPTION (the model uses 1.8%), with 0.5–0.9% as upside once bank quotes confirm eligibility [A1 §3.9; MODEL].

Eating out grows unevenly. Food and beverage services grew 27.1% (2023) and 10.9% (2024) in real terms VERIFIED [A1 §1 #10], and 3.1% in preliminary 2025 data, with restaurants at only 1.8% VERIFIED [A3 §2 #25]. Accommodation and food together rose 13.9% in Jan–Aug 2026 VERIFIED, but that line includes hotels and Armstat has not published the food-service line for 2026, so the 2026 trend for eating out alone is unknown [A3 §1 #9]. Net entries of Russian citizens (+65,750 in Jan–Aug 2026) ESTIMATE help central Yerevan cafés, depend on Armenia–Russia relations and stay out of base cases [A1 §1 #11].

2. The coffee market

Volume is flat; growth appears to be in to-go espresso. Apparent consumption was about 11.6 k t green-bean equivalent in 2025 (12.4 k t in 2019), about 3.8 kg per person ESTIMATE; FAOSTAT's 8.25 kg REPORTED conflicts, probably because it does not net out re-exports [A2 §2 #6–8]. Robusta-heavy Indonesia and Vietnam supplied about 89% of green imports by weight ESTIMATE (computed from VERIFIED Comtrade volumes) [A2 §2 #9], mostly for jezve coffee (սուրճ) at home and in offices. Espresso-type coffee is perhaps 10–15% of volume ASSUMPTION. Out-of-home espresso appears to be growing faster than the flat total ESTIMATE, but no survey measures the split [A2 §3.2]. Coffee House owns to-go: it claims 57 branches and lists 52, including the M4 Drive-Thru, Abovyan and Tsaghkadzor VERIFIED (company claim) [A2 §2 #14], and opened the premium sub-brand FEIN in September 2025 REPORTED [A2 §3.3].

American coffee chains are absent; Starbucks is circling. Starbucks has no store. The Economy Minister held exploratory talks with Starbucks-Alshaya Group Eurasia on 8 October 2025 REPORTED [A2 §2 #21], and World Coffee Portal reported Alshaya's first Georgian store in July 2026 REPORTED (paywalled; A3 could not confirm it independently) [A2 §7 #19; A3 §3.1]. Starbucks ready-to-drink coffee already sells at SAS for 1,250 AMD per 220 ml VERIFIED [A4 §2 #25]. Stars Coffee named Armenia as its probable first foreign market in 2022, but its October 2026 store list shows only Russian cities; there is no evidence of Coffee Like, Costa, Tim Hortons or Dunkin' outlets [A2 §3.4]. American QSR brands are present (KFC, Burger King, Wendy's; section 3). A2 places an "affordable premium" cappuccino at 1,400–1,600 AMD for 350 ml ASSUMPTION, 40–60% above the mass leader and 15–25% below the premium chains [A2 §3.6].

Drive-thru and fuel-station coffee on the M4

The only known drive-thru is Coffee House's. It sits at about official km 12.8 at Ptghni/Balahovit, next to a TumOil LPG station, on the Sevan-bound side ESTIMATE [A3 §3.3]. The company lists 08:30–24:00 VERIFIED (company claim), but Yandex shows 09:00–23:00, so the hours are unconfirmed [A3 §2 #2; A2 §7 #11]. It opened by December 2024 ESTIMATE [A3 §2 #3]. It is rated 4.9★ on 123 ratings REPORTED, yet its 17 written reviews average 3.76★ ESTIMATE, and 4 of the 5 written in June–September 2026 give 1–2★ for 20–60-minute waits and wrong orders REPORTED [A3 §2 #4]. A2's fact-check dates some of these complaints to 2025 while A3's dated review log places them in 2026; the year matters less than the pattern [A2 §7; A3 §3.3]. No other drive-thru was found, but the news sweep was not run, so "only one" is an ESTIMATE [A3 §2 #1]. Coffee House has not opened a second drive-thru in about two years, which may mean the economics are marginal ASSUMPTION [A3 §4 #10].

Fuel retailers are moving into coffee. Max Oil has about 11 Max Cafes across its 56 stations, including both sides of km 12.2 VERIFIED (fact-check corrected from 11/12) [A3 §2 #17]. Flash (54 stations) expanded its Express Shops with coffee and "Hotsters" in January 2026, including Tsaghkadzor, and shows an Express Cafe "opening soon" VERIFIED [A3 §2 #18–19]. CPS claims 101 stations, lists 53 (including Verin Ptghni, Abovyan, Hrazdan, Sevan and Dilijan) and shows no food format VERIFIED [A3 §2 #20]. Shell's Armenian site markets a "Shell Café" VERIFIED; OSM shows three Shell stations, all in Yerevan [A3 §3.4]. There are 37 fuel stations within 200 m of the M4 between the Yerevan edge and Sevan W REPORTED [B1 §2 #29], and four coffee points within 2.3 km at the Yerevan exit (km 10.5–12.8) REPORTED [A3 §1 #3]. No fuel-station coffee prices were found online.

3. QSR and convenience competitors

KFC has 14 restaurants, one of them suburban (Homplex, Proshyan) VERIFIED; none is known to have a lane ESTIMATE, and 13 of the 14 open at 10:00, so KFC does not compete at breakfast [A3 §2 #5]. Wendy's opened in central Yerevan in December 2025 VERIFIED and its franchisee has agreed to open 20 by 2030 REPORTED; the number open in October 2026 could not be checked [A3 §2 #9–10; A3 §1 #5]. Burger King has about five outlets REPORTED [A3 §2 #8], and no McDonald's appears in Wikipedia's current or former country lists or in OSM REPORTED [A5 §2 #18]. A mainstream QSR meal costs about 2,450–3,200 AMD ($6.77–8.84) at list prices ESTIMATE; the fact-check raised the low end from 1,700 AMD, which was a Glovo promotion [A3 §2 #16].

The Glovo menus of KFC, Mr. Gyros, Karas and Grill.am list no hot coffee, Coffee House sells sweets and Max Cafe sells hot dogs [A3 §1 #8; A4 §3.8]. The coffee-plus-food gap is narrower than that suggests: Tashir already serves omelettes at 1,000–1,500 AMD and its Glovo menu carries a 40 AMD "coffee cup" packaging fee, which implies it sells coffee in store REPORTED [A3 §3.2], and SAS Food Court sells omelettes at 550–1,450 AMD REPORTED [A4 §3.8]. Yerevan City has about 63–66 branches, two each in Abovyan and Hrazdan, and runs in-store food courts VERIFIED [A3 §2 #23]. Delivery (Yandex Eats, Glovo with 40%-off promotions capped at 3,000 AMD REPORTED) is a strong substitute in the suburbs and a weak one on the road [A3 §1 #10; A3 §3.7]. At the destination, lakeside restaurants line the Sevan bypass (km 60–73) REPORTED [A3 §3.4].

4. Food: what people eat and what travels in a car

Bread and flour supply 53% of adult energy, and 40% of adults ate cakes, pastries or biscuits on the survey day VERIFIED [A4 §2 #1, #3]. In a 2019 Yerevan survey pastry was the most-eaten fast food, and fast food was eaten about once a week by a 90% university-educated sample VERIFIED [A4 §2 #7–8]. No survey measures breakfast eaten out; Glovo's "Breakfast" filter returned one store of 33 REPORTED [A4 §3.1]. Local anchors are very low: a ponchik costs 130–150 AMD and a 175 g gata (գաթա) 300 AMD at Grand Candy, and a supermarket sandwich 1,090–1,350 AMD VERIFIED [A4 §2 #13, #17, #20]. A US donut at 800–1,000 AMD would cost 5–7 times a ponchik ESTIMATE. A4 recommends food at 400–1,600 AMD ASSUMPTION [A4 §1 #4, #11].

Across the region, drive-thru is led by burger and chicken chains on urban arterials, and roadside coffee belongs to fuel retailers. Cold does not stop lane use (Kazakhstan has drive-thrus in Astana and Karaganda), but it shrinks Sevan-road leisure traffic [A5 §1 #7]. A5 rates the chance of reaching base-case unit economics at about 25–35% for a standalone premium unit on the open M4, 50–60% for a lane-plus-walk-up hybrid on a Yerevan exit arterial and 45–55% for a fuel-station hybrid ASSUMPTION, with medium-low confidence; these rank options and are not model inputs [A5 §4].

Capture realism. C6 puts mature capture on the M4 at 0.3–1.3% of same-side light vehicles (base ≈0.7%) ESTIMATE [C6 §2 #49]. On S2's base of about 11,000, a 0.75% capture gives about 83 drive-thru transactions a day before walk-ins ESTIMATE [C6 §3.6], in line with A2's 80–150 a day for a well-sited unit ASSUMPTION [A2 §4 #8]. The model's base case assumes far more. Its 1.8% capture of accessible vehicles equals about 2.4% of same-side light vehicles ESTIMATE [C6 §2 #50] and produces 150–241 transactions a day at a 2,600–2,984 AMD ($7.18–8.24) average ticket over years 1–5 [MODEL]. C6 recommends cutting the base capture to 0.6% on the model's basis until counts at the Coffee House drive-thru justify more [C6 §4 #1].

What the evidence-based volume means for the model. On the model's base cost structure, EBITDA breaks even at about 150 transactions a day at a 2,691 AMD ($7.44) average ticket, the model's year-2 price; the conservative case needs about 195 a day at 2,266 AMD, and base-case EBIT needs about 206 a day ESTIMATE [MODEL]. Neither the base nor the conservative case pays back within five years: base-case IRR is −3.6% including residual value and NPV is −151 M AMD (−$418k) on a 252 M AMD ($696k) investment [MODEL]. The conservative case, whose 64–95 transactions a day are closest to the evidence, loses 48–65 M AMD ($132–180k) of EBITDA every year [MODEL]. The 80–150 transactions a day the evidence supports therefore sit at or below EBITDA break-even even at the model's ticket. At the 1,540–1,860 AMD order estimated from local prices ESTIMATE [A4 §3.11], break-even volume would be higher still. Most model inputs (capex, rent, traffic, capture, ticket) are still placeholders, so these outputs are provisional; the financial chapter tests them.

8. Market gaps and the founder's premise

The evidence contradicts three of the four elements of "premium American-style drive-thru on the M4" (premium, American-style, the open M4) and is consistent with, but has not measured, the fourth, the drive-thru format. The claim to be first is already false, and at the volumes the evidence supports the modelled unit does not cover its costs.

Where the opening is.

  1. Speed and accuracy. The incumbent's customers report 20–60-minute waits, against a US average service time of 4:04 and 89.3% order accuracy; US satisfaction falls to 84.9% once a visit passes 10 minutes REPORTED [C6 §1 #4; C6 §2 #39–40]. Coffee House can close this gap by adding staff, so the lead is measured in months ASSUMPTION [A3 §3.7].
  2. Coffee plus hot Armenian hand-held food for drivers. No operator combines barista coffee and real hot food with car service ASSUMPTION [A3 §1 #8; A4 §3.8]. If 30–45% of orders include food ASSUMPTION, A4 estimates an average order of about 1,540–1,860 AMD ($4.25–5.14) ESTIMATE [A4 §3.11]. The gap is narrow: Tashir already sells omelettes and appears to sell coffee in store REPORTED [A3 §3.2], and Flash's Express Cafe and Max Cafe could close it on the highway within 6–12 months ASSUMPTION [A3 §4 #5].
  3. The morning daypart. KFC opens at 10:00 and the Coffee House drive-thru at 08:30 at the earliest, so 07:00–08:30 appears uncontested by a lane [A3 §1 #1; A3 §2 #5], and Glovo lists one breakfast store [A4 §3.1]. Against that, Tashir (omelettes 1,000–1,500 AMD) and SAS Food Court (550–1,450 AMD) already sell hot breakfast items REPORTED [A3 §3.2; A4 §3.8], and no breakfast-out habit has been measured, so ramp-up is likely to be slow ASSUMPTION [A4 §3.1].
  4. Site logic. Year-round volume sits near Yerevan, and the weekday morning coffee peak runs on the Yerevan-bound side ASSUMPTION [B1 §4 #4], where Max Cafe at km 12.2 already trades [A3 §3.4]. Interchange parcels (Abovyan km 15.7–16.7, Charentsavan 31.1, Hrazdan 39.3 and 47.4; positions ESTIMATE from OSM) can serve both directions if drivers accept the exit detour, which intercepts must test ASSUMPTION [B1 §4 #3]. A CPS or independent CNG forecourt beyond the km 10–13 cluster inherits stopping traffic [A3 §4 #2–3].
Decision. Replace "premium American-style drive-thru on the M4" with a testable concept: a fast, car-first coffee-and-hot-food unit with an Armenian menu at affordable-premium prices, on a Yerevan exit corridor, an interchange or a fuel forecourt. Treat any revenue case with an average order above about 2,000 AMD ($5.53) or more than about 150 transactions a day as a stretch case until field counts support it [A4 §4 #3; A2 §4 #8; C6 §4 #1]. By this test the model's base case is itself a stretch case: it assumes a 2,600–2,984 AMD average order and 150–241 transactions a day [MODEL], and its 1.8% capture equals about 2.4% of same-side light vehicles against C6's 0.3–1.3% ESTIMATE [C6 §2 #49–50]. C6 recommends a 0.6% base capture on the model's basis until counts at the Coffee House drive-thru justify more [C6 §4 #1]. The resolved concept already uses a base order of about 1,850 AMD ESTIMATE and commits no build capex until field evidence shows a site that can sustain at least 150 transactions a day [STRATEGY:concept]. Sign no land or lease before the checks below.

What we know vs what we estimate

  • Known: 41.2% of households own a car; Coffee House runs a drive-thru on the M4 at mass prices, and its reviews report long summer waits; mass to-go coffee costs 800–1,000 AMD and premium chains stop at about 1,950; Max Oil and Flash are adding cafés along the corridor; the last official M4 count is from 2008; the average inbound tourist stays 29 nights.
  • Estimated or assumed: that Coffee House's is the only drive-thru (the news sweep is outstanding); 2025 traffic by segment; seasonality of 1.5× near Yerevan and 2.3–2.6× beyond Hrazdan; capture of 0.3–1.3% and 80–150 transactions a day; an average order of 1,540–1,860 AMD with food; the short-stay share of tourists (about 16%); segment sizes; whether people will use a lane for breakfast; how fast incumbents copy; and every model output, including the break-even of about 150 a day, which rests on placeholder inputs.

Field checks before any land commitment

  1. Traffic counts. Request 2019–2026 M4 counter data from the Road Department Fund and MTAI, and Road Police camera counts. Run 7-day classified video counts at six screenlines and the shortlisted parcel in summer and winter, with plate matching and stop-rate counts at existing stops: about 6.5–12.7 M AMD ($18–35k), or 1.4–2.9 M AMD ($4–8k) for a single-parcel pre-screen ASSUMPTION [B1 §5].
  2. Observe the Coffee House Drive-Thru on three weekdays and two weekend days, three dayparts each, summer and winter: cars per hour, order-to-pickup time, queue length, direction of travel, and the actual opening hours [A2 §5; A3 §5].
  3. Intercept and household survey of at least 600 people in Yerevan, Kotayk and Gegharkunik, with quotas by car ownership and age: coffee frequency, breakfast out, drive-thru use, acceptable wait, exit-detour tolerance and a price ladder at 1,000 / 1,500 / 2,000 / 2,500 AMD; plus 150–200 intercepts per roadside stop per season [A1 §5; A4 §5; A5 §5; B1 §5].
  4. Mystery shopping at Max Cafe km 12.2 (both sides), Flash and CPS in Sevan, Abovyan and Hrazdan, Gas #1 at km 51 and Tashir in Abovyan: coffee prices, cup sizes, machine type, breakfast items, toilets and CNG dwell time. Photograph menu boards at 15 brands in store and compare with Glovo [A3 §5; A2 §5].
  5. News sweep of 2018–2026 drive-thru openings and closures before treating "only one" as final [A3 §5].
  6. Re-run the model with C6's 0.6% base capture, segment-A seasonality for a Yerevan-end site and an evidence-based order of about 1,850 AMD, and get bank acquiring quotes, before any capital decision [C6 §4 #1; B3 §3.7; STRATEGY:concept; A1 §3.9].
