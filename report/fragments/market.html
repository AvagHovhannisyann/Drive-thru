Phase 1 · Market research

The Armenian market: demand, competition and drive-thru fit

People in Armenia do buy coffee from the car, but the opening the founder assumed is already taken. Coffee House has run a drive-thru on the M4 at about km 12.8 since late 2024, selling cappuccino at 800–1,200 AMD ($2.21–3.32) REPORTED, and Max Oil runs fuel-station cafés on both carriageways at km 12.2. The evidence supports a narrower opening: faster and more accurate service, plus hot Armenian hand-held food at affordable-premium prices, on sites with year-round commuter traffic near Yerevan or on a fuel forecourt. It does not support a premium, American-branded unit on the open highway.

1. Who can buy: incomes, cars and payments

Incomes are low against an American ticket. The average gross wage was 312,486 AMD ($863) a month in Jan–Aug 2026 VERIFIED [A1 §2]. Yerevan averaged 357,583 AMD ($988) in Q2 2026. Along the M4, Kotayk paid 208,210 AMD (58% of Yerevan) and Gegharkunik 158,949 AMD (44%) VERIFIED [A1 §3.3]. A 2,000 AMD drink costs about 1.5 hours of net work for an average earner and 2.9 hours for a food-service worker ESTIMATE [A1 §3.3]. On decile average incomes, eight such drinks a month would take at least 11.6% of per-capita income for every decile except the top one ESTIMATE [A1 §3.7]. Urban residents report spending 2,293 AMD a month on all food away from home, 3.5% of consumption VERIFIED. National catering turnover works out to about 8,400 AMD per resident per month ESTIMATE, so the household survey misses tourists, business spending and high earners [A1 §3.6]. Poverty is 21.7% nationally, 16.9% in Yerevan, 24.8% in Kotayk and 27.6% in Gegharkunik VERIFIED; Kotayk's confidence interval (13.9–35.8%) is too wide to treat the gap with Yerevan as robust [A1 §7 #20].

Demand is concentrated in Yerevan. The capital holds 1,149.5 k of 3,103.9 k residents (37.0%) VERIFIED [A1 §2]. The M4 corridor towns hold 167,262 people VERIFIED [B3 §2 #27], and the three north-eastern districts that feed the M4 exit (Nor Nork, Kanaker-Zeytun, Avan) hold 272.6 k VERIFIED [A1 §3.1]. A highway unit therefore lives on trips that start in Yerevan.

Car access is modest. 41.2% of households own a car VERIFIED [A1 §7 #19]; the Caucasus Barometer gives 46% nationally and 42% in Yerevan VERIFIED [A5 §2 #3]. About 954 k vehicles of all types are registered, roughly 310 per 1,000 residents REPORTED, and the source's reference year (2024 or 2025) is unresolved [A1 §7 #23]. Taxis carried 28.1% of passengers on commercial transport in 2024 VERIFIED. That statistic covers paid transport only and excludes private cars and walking (A1 fact-check correction) [A1 §7 #22]: it shows that ride-hailing is large, and says nothing about how many trips are made without a car. Car-free customers cannot use a lane, so a walk-up window is a design requirement.

Inflation and FX. CPI was 4.3% y/y in September 2026, with food at 6.2% and restaurants and hotels at 5.4% REPORTED; the CBA raised its policy rate to 6.75% on 15 September REPORTED [A1 §3.4]. The retail price of coffee rose 14.7% in 2025 to 5,549 AMD/kg VERIFIED [A1 §3.4], and AI-95 petrol is at a record 630 AMD/l ($1.74) REPORTED, a drag on leisure driving [A1 §3.8]. The dram is at 361.93 per USD VERIFIED, close to its 12-month strongest level (range 361.81–383.05); A1 recommends stress-testing 345–390 ASSUMPTION [A1 §3.5].

Payments are ready. Armenia has 5.47 m payment cards, 86.9% of them NFC-enabled VERIFIED. The average card purchase at a physical terminal fell from 6,654 AMD (Q1 2024) to 3,473 AMD ($9.60) in Q2 2026 ESTIMATE, so people now pay by card for coffee-sized amounts. Apple Pay works with 17 issuers VERIFIED; Google Pay is confirmed only for Ameriabank [A1 §3.9]. The CBA's acquiring-fee cap (0.5% ArCa, 0.9% Visa/Mastercard from 1 March 2026) applies only to businesses with annual turnover up to 150 M AMD ($414k) REPORTED. That is about 411k AMD a day; a unit at target volume would exceed it, so the base case should use a 1.5–2% fee ASSUMPTION [A1 §7 #15].

Eating out is growing, unevenly. Food and beverage services grew in real terms by 18.9% (2022), 27.1% (2023) and 10.9% (2024) VERIFIED [A1 §3.12], slowed to 3.1% in preliminary 2025 data (restaurants +1.8%) VERIFIED [A3 §2 #25], and accommodation and food rose 13.9% in Jan–Aug 2026 VERIFIED [A3 §2 #26]. Net border entries of Russian citizens reached +65,750 in Jan–Aug 2026 ESTIMATE from verified border data. This mainly helps central Yerevan cafés and depends on Armenia–Russia relations, which worsened in 2026; A1 advises keeping it out of base cases [A1 §3.12].

2. The coffee market

Volume is flat; to-go espresso is where growth is. Apparent coffee consumption was about 11.6 k t green-bean equivalent in 2025, against 12.4 k t in 2019, or about 3.8 kg per person ESTIMATE. FAOSTAT (via Helgi) reports 8.25 kg REPORTED; A2 attributes the conflict to re-exports and uses 3.8–4.6 kg [A2 §2 #6–8]. Indonesia and Vietnam, both robusta-heavy, supplied 89% of green imports by weight VERIFIED [A2 §2 #9], most of it ground for jezve coffee (սուրճ) at home and in offices. Espresso-type coffee is perhaps 10–15% of volume ASSUMPTION, and no survey measures the split [A2 §3.2]. The green-coffee import price nearly tripled between 2019 and 2025 VERIFIED [A2 §2 #3].

Coffee House owns to-go. It claims 57 branches and lists 52: 41 in Yerevan city plus the airport, the M4 Drive-Thru, Abovyan, Tsaghkadzor and regional towns VERIFIED (company claim) [A2 §2 #14]. In September 2025 it opened FEIN, a premium specialty sub-brand with its own roaster REPORTED [A2 §3.3].

The American brands are absent, and one is circling. Starbucks has no store in Armenia. On 8 October 2025 the Economy Minister held exploratory talks with Starbucks-Alshaya Group Eurasia REPORTED (downgraded from VERIFIED because the sources are news reports) [A2 §2 #21]. World Coffee Portal reported Alshaya's first Georgian store in Tbilisi on 17 July 2026 REPORTED; the article is paywalled and A3 could not confirm it independently [A2 §1; A3 §3.1]. Starbucks ready-to-drink coffee already sells in SAS supermarkets at 1,250 AMD per 220 ml VERIFIED [A4 §2 #25]. Stars Coffee named Armenia as a likely first foreign market in 2022 and has not opened. There is no evidence of Coffee Like, Costa, Tim Hortons or Dunkin' outlets; Dunkin' operates in Georgia [A2 §3.4]. Foreign brands that are present are Cofix (from January 2024), Surf Coffee and Coffeeshop Company (three stores each), Segafredo and Paul [A2 §1]. A2 places an "affordable premium" cappuccino at 1,400–1,600 AMD for 350 ml and an iced latte at 1,600–1,800 AMD ASSUMPTION: 40–60% above the mass leader and 15–25% below the premium chains [A2 §3.6].

Drive-thru and fuel-station coffee on the M4

The only known drive-thru is Coffee House's. It sits at about official km 12.8 at Ptghni/Balahovit, on the Sevan-bound side, 31 m from a TumOil LPG station. The company lists 08:30–24:00 VERIFIED; Yandex showed "until 23:00" on 10 October 2026 [A3 §2 #2; A2 §2 #16]. It opened by December 2024 ESTIMATE [A3 §2 #3]. It is rated 4.9★ on 123 ratings, yet its 17 written reviews average 3.76★, and 4 of the 5 written in June–September 2026 give 1–2★ for 20–60-minute waits, wrong orders and rude staff REPORTED [A3 §2 #4]. OpenStreetMap has no café tagged as a drive-through anywhere in Armenia. The news sweep for other drive-thrus was not run, so "only one" remains an estimate ESTIMATE [A3 §2 #1]. Coffee House has not opened a second drive-thru in about two years, which may mean the economics are marginal ASSUMPTION [A3 §4].

Fuel retailers are moving into coffee. Max Oil lists 56 stations, 10 with a tagged Max Cafe, plus the km 12.2 Yerevan-bound unit announced in January 2026, so about 11 VERIFIED (corrected in fact-check from 11/12) [A3 §2 #17]. Flash lists 54 stations; it expanded its Express Shops (coffee, "Hotsters", Wi-Fi) in January 2026, including Tsaghkadzor, and shows an Express Cafe as "opening soon" VERIFIED [A3 §2 #18–19]. CPS claims 101 stations but lists 53, including Verin Ptghni, Abovyan, Hrazdan, Sevan, Varser and Dilijan, and shows no food format online VERIFIED (conflicting counts) [A3 §2 #20]. Shell's Armenian site markets a "Shell Café" with Signature Espresso and Lungo blends VERIFIED; the station count is unknown, and OSM shows three, all in Yerevan [A3 §3.4]. Between the Yerevan edge and Sevan W there are 37 fuel stations within 200 m of the road REPORTED (OSM, incomplete) [B1 §2 #29]. Within 2.3 km at the Yerevan exit (km 10.5–12.8) there are four coffee points: Ninja coffee, Max Cafe on both sides and the Coffee House Drive-Thru [A3 §1]. No fuel-station coffee prices were found online.

3. QSR and convenience competitors relevant to a roadside stop

KFC has 14 restaurants: 12 in Yerevan city, one at Homplex Hyper Mall in Proshyan and one in Vanadzor VERIFIED. Its ordering platform flags drive_thru:false; the absence of a physical lane is an ESTIMATE, and 13 of the 14 open at 10:00, so KFC does not compete at breakfast [A3 §2 #5]. Burger King has about five outlets REPORTED. Wendy's opened its first restaurant on Abovyan Street in December 2025 VERIFIED, and its franchisee has agreed to open 20 by 2030 REPORTED [A3 §2 #8–10]. No McDonald's was found [A5 §2 #18].

Local QSR sets low anchors: KFC Zinger 1,300 AMD and combos 2,800–3,200; Tashir burger 1,300–1,350, Adjarian khachapuri 1,500 and omelettes 1,000–1,500; Karas shawarma 1,260–1,740 REPORTED. A mainstream QSR meal costs 1,700–3,200 AMD ($4.70–8.84) ESTIMATE [A3 §1]. None of the QSR chains' delivery menus lists hot coffee, Coffee House sells sweets, and Max Cafe sells hot dogs [A3 §1; A4 §3.8]. Yerevan City has 63 supermarkets with food courts, including two each in Abovyan and Hrazdan; SAS has 12 (nine open 24/7) and Zovq 28 VERIFIED [A3 §2 #23]. Menu.am moved onto Yandex Eats in November 2024 VERIFIED and Glovo runs 40%-off promotions on orders over 5,000 AMD REPORTED, a strong substitute in the suburbs and a weak one on the road [A3 §3.6]. At the destination, lakeside restaurants line the Sevan bypass (km 60–73) and about 47 café and restaurant points cluster near Dilijan REPORTED [A3 §3.4].

4. Food: what people eat and what travels in a car

Armenians eat dough first. Bread and flour products supply 53% of adult energy, and 40% of adults ate cakes, pastries or biscuits on the survey day VERIFIED [A4 §2 #1, #3]. In a 2019 Yerevan survey, pastry was the most-eaten fast food (76 g/day) and fast food was eaten about once a week; 90% of that sample was university-educated, so the true rate is probably lower VERIFIED [A4 §2 #7–8]. No survey measures breakfast eaten out. On Glovo the "Breakfast" filter returned one store (Jazzve) of 33 REPORTED, and researchers describe breakfast skipping as widespread. Breakfast-to-go looks under-supplied, and the habit is unproven [A4 §3.1].

Local price anchors are very low: a Grand Candy ponchik costs 130–150 AMD, a 175 g gata (գաթա) 300 AMD and a supermarket sandwich 1,090–1,350 AMD VERIFIED; lahmajo sells at 480–1,000 AMD REPORTED [A4 §2 #13, #15, #17, #20]. A US donut at 800–1,000 AMD would cost 5–7 times a ponchik ESTIMATE. A4 recommends a food band of 400–1,600 AMD ASSUMPTION [A4 §1; §3.3].

The regional pattern is consistent. Where drive-thru exists it is led by burger and chicken chains on urban arterials, and roadside coffee belongs to fuel retailers. Cold weather does not stop lane use (Kazakhstan has tagged drive-thrus in Astana and Karaganda), but it cuts the leisure traffic a Sevan-road site depends on [A5 §1]. A5 ranks the options by subjective probability of reaching base-case unit economics: a standalone premium drive-only unit on the open M4 about 25–35%, a drive-thru-led hybrid with walk-up and parking on a Yerevan exit arterial about 50–60%, and a fuel-station hybrid about 45–55% ASSUMPTION. These rank options; they are not model inputs [A5 §4].

6. M4 traffic, seasonality and tourism

The traffic base is old and uncertain. The newest published M4 count is from 2008: 21,505 veh/day at the Yerevan exit (km 10+320), down from 25,386 in 2007 VERIFIED. The Dilijan tunnel counter is erratic (783 veh/day in 2008) [B1 §2 #1–2]. Counters probably still run: ADB cites growth of 12% a year on the M-1 and 9% on the M-5 near Yerevan in 2016–21 VERIFIED, but no M4 series is public [B1 §2 #5–6]. Lake Sevan's coastal water reaches about 21 °C only in July and August (21.2 °C in both months of 2026) VERIFIED [B3 §2 #28], so the swimming peak is about 8–12 weekends ASSUMPTION. Sevan's January mean is −6.1 °C, with about 32 days a year of 1 cm or more of snowfall ESTIMATE (ERA5 reanalysis) [B3 §2 #30].

Tourism is an upside layer. Armenia counted 2,263,642 inbound tourists in 2025 and 1,041,598 in H1 2026, up 14.8% VERIFIED [B3 §2 #1, #3]. The average stay is 29 nights; 41% are Russian citizens and 14% Armenian citizens, and only about 16% (≈0.37 m) fit a short-stay sightseeing profile ESTIMATE [B3 §2 #7–8]. Gegharkunik, which holds all of Lake Sevan, earned 1.8% of national hotel revenue in 2025 and 1.2% of accommodation and food turnover in 2024 VERIFIED [B3 §1]. International tourists are about 4–9% of passes on the Hrazdan–Sevan segment ESTIMATE [B3 §3.6]. B3's bottom-up count of domestic leisure trips (0.15–0.75 m car round trips a year) equals 6–24% of B1's base AADT on that segment ESTIMATE, well below what a "leisure highway" implies. Either B1's estimate there is high, or the corridor is more regional than leisure; only counts can settle it [B3 §3.8]. Bagratashen, the main Georgia crossing, recorded 1.40 m entries in 2025 VERIFIED, implying about 1,200–2,100 Georgia-linked vehicles a day on Sevan–Dilijan ESTIMATE [B3 §1].

8. Market gaps and the founder's premise

The evidence contradicts three of the four elements of "premium American-style drive-thru on the M4" (premium, American-style, the open M4) and supports the fourth, the drive-thru format itself. The claim to be first is already false.

Where the opening is.

  1. Speed and accuracy. The incumbent's customers report 20–60-minute waits. US benchmarks are a 4:04 service time and 89.3% order accuracy, and satisfaction falls to 84.9% once a visit passes 10 minutes REPORTED (Intouch 2026) [C6 §2 #39–40; C6 §1]. Coffee House can close this gap by adding staff, so it is a lead measured in months [A3 §3.7].
  2. Coffee plus hot Armenian hand-held food in a car-first stop. No operator combines barista coffee with hot savoury food for drivers [A3 §1; A4 §3.8]. With 30–45% of orders including food, A4 estimates an average order of about 1,540–1,860 AMD ($4.25–5.14) ESTIMATE [A4 §3.11]. Flash's Express Cafe and Max Cafe's "fast food" push could close this gap within 6–12 months [A3 §4].
  3. The morning daypart. KFC opens at 10:00 and Glovo lists one breakfast store, but no breakfast-out habit has been measured, so ramp-up is likely to be slow ASSUMPTION [A3 §3.1; A4 §3.1].
  4. Site logic. Year-round commuter volume sits near Yerevan on the Yerevan-bound side. Interchange parcels (Abovyan km 15.7 and 16.7, Charentsavan/Fantan 31.1, Hrazdan 39.3 and 47.4) can serve both directions [B1 §4], and a CPS or independent CNG forecourt beyond the km 10–13 cluster could inherit stopping traffic [A3 §4].
Decision. Replace "premium American-style drive-thru on the M4" with a testable concept: a fast, car-first coffee-and-hot-food unit with an Armenian menu and affordable-premium prices, on a Yerevan exit corridor, an interchange or a fuel forecourt. Treat any revenue case with an average order above about 2,000 AMD ($5.53) or more than about 150 transactions a day as a stretch case until field counts support it [A4 §4; A2 §4; C6 §4]. Do not sign land or a lease before the checks below.

What we know vs what we estimate

Known (verified or reported)

  • 41.2% of households own a car; 42% in Yerevan on the Caucasus Barometer measure.
  • Coffee House runs the only known drive-thru, on the M4, at mass prices, with documented long waits in summer 2026.
  • Mass to-go coffee costs 800–1,000 AMD; premium chains stop at about 1,950 AMD.
  • Max Oil and Flash are adding cafés along the corridor; CPS shows no food format.
  • The last official M4 count is 21,505 veh/day at the Yerevan exit in 2008.
  • Most "tourists" are long-stay visitors; Gegharkunik takes 1.8% of national hotel revenue.

Estimated or assumed

  • 2025 traffic by segment (33–54k AADT at the Yerevan edge) and same-side light vehicles (about 11k between Abovyan and Charentsavan).
  • Seasonality: about 1.5× near Yerevan and 2.3–2.6× beyond Hrazdan.
  • Capture of 0.3–1.3% of same-side light vehicles, and 80–150 transactions a day for a well-sited unit.
  • An average order of about 1,500–1,900 AMD with food, and willingness to pay for "premium".
  • Segment sizes for commuters and leisure trips, and whether people will use a lane for breakfast.
  • How fast Coffee House, Max Oil and Flash copy speed and hot food.

Field checks needed before any land commitment

  1. Traffic counts. Send a formal data request to the Road Department Fund and MTAI for 2019–2026 M4 counter data and Road Police camera counts. Run 7-day classified video counts at six screenlines plus the shortlisted parcel, in July–August and in winter, with plate matching and stop-rate counts at existing fuel and café stops. Cost about 6.5–12.7 M AMD ($18–35k); a single-parcel pre-screen about 1.4–2.9 M AMD ($4–8k) ASSUMPTION [B1 §5].
  2. Coffee House Drive-Thru observation. Three weekdays and two weekend days, three dayparts each, in summer and winter: cars per hour, order-to-pickup time, queue length and direction of travel [A2 §5].
  3. Intercept and household survey. At least 600 respondents in Yerevan, Kotayk and Gegharkunik, with quotas by car ownership and age: coffee frequency, breakfast out, stated drive-thru use, acceptable wait, and a price ladder at 1,000 / 1,500 / 2,000 / 2,500 AMD. Add 150–200 intercepts per stop per season at existing roadside stops [A1 §5; A4 §5; A5 §5; B1 §5].
  4. Mystery shopping. Record coffee prices, cup sizes, machine type, food range and toilets at Max Cafe km 12.2 (both sides), Flash and CPS in Sevan, Abovyan and Hrazdan, and Gas #1 at km 51; time at least 50 CNG fills. Photograph in-store menu boards at 15 brands, two branches each, and compare with Glovo prices [A3 §5; A2 §5].
  5. News sweep. Search Armenian and Russian news from 2018 to 2026 for other drive-thru openings and closures before treating "only one" as final [A3 §5].
