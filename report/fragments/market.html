Phase 1 · Market research

The Armenian market: demand, competition and drive-thru fit

People in Armenia do buy coffee from the car, but the opening the founder assumed is already taken. Coffee House has run a drive-thru on the M4 at about km 12.8 since late 2024, selling cappuccino at 800–1,200 AMD ($2.21–3.32) REPORTED, and Max Oil runs fuel-station cafés on both carriageways at km 12.2. The evidence supports a narrower opening: faster, more accurate service and hot Armenian hand-held food at affordable-premium prices, on sites with year-round commuter traffic near Yerevan or on a fuel forecourt. It does not support a premium, American-branded unit on the open highway.

1. Who can buy: incomes, cars and payments

Incomes are low against an American ticket. The average gross wage was 312,486 AMD ($863) a month in Jan–Aug 2026 VERIFIED [A1 §2]. Kotayk and Gegharkunik, the M4 marzes, pay 58% and 44% of Yerevan's 357,583 AMD ($988) VERIFIED [A1 §3.3]. A 2,000 AMD drink costs about 1.5 hours of net work for an average earner ESTIMATE [A1 §3.3]. Urban residents report spending 2,293 AMD a month on all food away from home, 3.5% of consumption VERIFIED; national catering turnover implies about 8,400 AMD per resident ESTIMATE, so the survey misses tourists, business spending and high earners [A1 §3.6]. Poverty is 16.9% in Yerevan, 24.8% in Kotayk and 27.6% in Gegharkunik VERIFIED, though Kotayk's confidence interval (13.9–35.8%) is too wide to call the gap robust [A1 §7 #20].

Demand sits in Yerevan, and car access is modest. Yerevan holds 37.0% of residents VERIFIED [A1 §2]; the M4 corridor towns hold 167,262 VERIFIED [B3 §2 #27], so a highway unit lives on trips that start in Yerevan. 41.2% of households own a car VERIFIED [A1 §7 #19]; the Caucasus Barometer gives 46% nationally and 42% in Yerevan VERIFIED [A5 §2 #3]. Taxis carried 28.1% of commercial-transport passengers in 2024 VERIFIED. The figure excludes private cars and walking (A1 fact-check correction), so it shows the weight of ride-hailing in paid transport and cannot be read as a car-free share [A1 §7 #22]. Car-free customers need a walk-up window.

Prices, FX and payments. CPI was 4.3% y/y in September 2026 and food 6.2% REPORTED [A1 §3.4]; the retail price of coffee rose 14.7% in 2025 VERIFIED [A1 §3.4]; AI-95 is at a record 630 AMD/l ($1.74) REPORTED, a drag on leisure driving [A1 §3.8]. The dram is at 361.93 per USD VERIFIED, near its 12-month strongest level; A1 recommends stress-testing 345–390 ASSUMPTION [A1 §3.5]. Of 5.47 m cards, 86.9% are NFC-enabled VERIFIED, and the average card purchase at a terminal fell from 6,654 AMD (Q1 2024) to 3,473 AMD ($9.60) in Q2 2026 ESTIMATE: people now pay by card for coffee-sized amounts. Apple Pay works with 17 issuers VERIFIED [A1 §3.9]. The CBA's acquiring-fee cap (0.5% ArCa, 0.9% Visa/Mastercard) applies only up to 150 M AMD ($414k) of annual turnover REPORTED, about 411k AMD a day. A unit at target volume would exceed it, so the base case should use a 1.5–2% fee ASSUMPTION [A1 §7 #15].

Eating out grows unevenly. Food and beverage services grew 27.1% (2023) and 10.9% (2024) in real terms VERIFIED [A1 §3.12], 3.1% in preliminary 2025 data VERIFIED [A3 §2 #25], and accommodation and food rose 13.9% in Jan–Aug 2026 VERIFIED [A3 §2 #26]. Net entries of Russian citizens (+65,750 in Jan–Aug 2026) ESTIMATE help central Yerevan cafés, depend on Armenia–Russia relations and stay out of base cases [A1 §3.12].

2. The coffee market

Volume is flat; growth is in to-go espresso. Apparent consumption was about 11.6 k t green-bean equivalent in 2025 (12.4 k t in 2019), about 3.8 kg per person ESTIMATE; FAOSTAT's 8.25 kg REPORTED conflicts, probably because it does not net out re-exports [A2 §2 #6–8]. Robusta-heavy Indonesia and Vietnam supplied 89% of green imports VERIFIED [A2 §2 #9], mostly for jezve coffee (սուրճ) at home and in offices. Espresso-type coffee is perhaps 10–15% of volume ASSUMPTION; no survey measures the split [A2 §3.2]. Coffee House owns to-go: it claims 57 branches and lists 52, including the M4 Drive-Thru, Abovyan and Tsaghkadzor VERIFIED (company claim) [A2 §2 #14], and opened the premium sub-brand FEIN in September 2025 REPORTED [A2 §3.3].

Starbucks ready-to-drink coffee already sells at SAS for 1,250 AMD per 220 ml VERIFIED [A4 §2 #25]. Stars Coffee announced Armenia in 2022 and never opened; there is no evidence of Coffee Like, Costa, Tim Hortons or Dunkin' outlets [A2 §3.4]. A2 places an "affordable premium" cappuccino at 1,400–1,600 AMD for 350 ml ASSUMPTION, 40–60% above the mass leader and 15–25% below the premium chains [A2 §3.6].

Drive-thru and fuel-station coffee on the M4

The only known drive-thru is Coffee House's. It sits at about official km 12.8 at Ptghni/Balahovit, on the Sevan-bound side next to a TumOil LPG station, and the company lists 08:30–24:00 VERIFIED (Yandex showed "until 23:00" on 10 October) [A3 §2 #2; A2 §2 #16]. It opened by December 2024 ESTIMATE [A3 §2 #3]. It is rated 4.9★ on 123 ratings, yet its 17 written reviews average 3.76★, and 4 of the 5 written in June–September 2026 give 1–2★ for 20–60-minute waits and wrong orders REPORTED [A3 §2 #4]. No other drive-thru was found, but the news sweep was not run, so "only one" is an ESTIMATE [A3 §2 #1]. Coffee House has not opened a second drive-thru in about two years, which may mean the economics are marginal ASSUMPTION [A3 §4].

Fuel retailers are moving into coffee. Max Oil has about 11 Max Cafes across its 56 stations, including both sides of km 12.2 VERIFIED (fact-check corrected from 11/12) [A3 §2 #17]. Flash (54 stations) expanded its Express Shops with coffee and "Hotsters" in January 2026, including Tsaghkadzor, and shows an Express Cafe "opening soon" VERIFIED [A3 §2 #18–19]. CPS claims 101 stations, lists 53 (including Verin Ptghni, Abovyan, Hrazdan, Sevan and Dilijan) and shows no food format VERIFIED [A3 §2 #20]. Shell's Armenian site markets a "Shell Café" VERIFIED; OSM shows three Shell stations, all in Yerevan [A3 §3.4]. There are 37 fuel stations within 200 m of the M4 between the Yerevan edge and Sevan W REPORTED [B1 §2 #29], and four coffee points within 2.3 km at the Yerevan exit (km 10.5–12.8) [A3 §1]. No fuel-station coffee prices were found online.

3. QSR and convenience competitors

KFC has 14 restaurants, one of them suburban (Homplex, Proshyan) VERIFIED; none is known to have a lane ESTIMATE, and 13 of the 14 open at 10:00, so KFC does not compete at breakfast [A3 §2 #5]. Wendy's opened in central Yerevan in December 2025 VERIFIED and its franchisee has agreed to open 20 by 2030 REPORTED [A3 §2 #9–10]; Burger King has about five outlets REPORTED and no McDonald's was found [A3 §2 #8; A5 §2 #18]. A mainstream QSR meal costs 1,700–3,200 AMD ($4.70–8.84) ESTIMATE [A3 §1]. No QSR delivery menu lists hot coffee, Coffee House sells sweets and Max Cafe sells hot dogs [A3 §1; A4 §3.8]. Yerevan City has 63 branches, two each in Abovyan and Hrazdan, and runs in-store food courts VERIFIED [A3 §2 #23]. Delivery (Yandex Eats, Glovo with 40%-off promotions) is a strong substitute in the suburbs and a weak one on the road [A3 §3.7]. At the destination, lakeside restaurants line the Sevan bypass (km 60–73) REPORTED [A3 §3.4].

4. Food: what people eat and what travels in a car

Bread and flour supply 53% of adult energy, and 40% of adults ate cakes, pastries or biscuits on the survey day VERIFIED [A4 §2 #1, #3]. In a 2019 Yerevan survey pastry was the most-eaten fast food, and fast food was eaten about once a week by a 90% university-educated sample VERIFIED [A4 §2 #7–8]. No survey measures breakfast eaten out; Glovo's "Breakfast" filter returned one store of 33 REPORTED [A4 §3.1]. Local anchors are very low: a ponchik costs 130–150 AMD and a 175 g gata (գաթա) 300 AMD at Grand Candy, and a supermarket sandwich 1,090–1,350 AMD VERIFIED [A4 §2 #13, #17, #20]. A US donut at 800–1,000 AMD would cost 5–7 times a ponchik ESTIMATE. A4 recommends food at 400–1,600 AMD ASSUMPTION [A4 §1; §3.3].

Across the region, drive-thru is led by burger and chicken chains on urban arterials, and roadside coffee belongs to fuel retailers. A5 rates the chance of reaching base-case unit economics at about 25–35% for a standalone premium unit on the open M4, 50–60% for a lane-plus-walk-up hybrid on a Yerevan exit arterial and 45–55% for a fuel-station hybrid ASSUMPTION; these rank options and are not model inputs [A5 §4].

8. Market gaps and the founder's premise

The evidence contradicts three of the four elements of "premium American-style drive-thru on the M4" (premium, American-style, the open M4) and supports the fourth, the drive-thru format. The claim to be first is already false.

Where the opening is.

  1. Speed and accuracy. The incumbent's customers report 20–60-minute waits, against a US average service time of 4:04 and 89.3% order accuracy; US satisfaction falls to 84.9% once a visit passes 10 minutes REPORTED [C6 §1; C6 §2 #39–40]. Coffee House can close this gap by adding staff, so the lead is measured in months [A3 §3.7].
  2. Coffee plus hot Armenian hand-held food for drivers. No operator combines the two [A3 §1; A4 §3.8]. With 30–45% of orders including food, A4 estimates an average order of about 1,540–1,860 AMD ($4.25–5.14) ESTIMATE [A4 §3.11]. Flash's Express Cafe and Max Cafe could close this gap within 6–12 months [A3 §4].
  3. The morning daypart. KFC opens at 10:00 and Glovo lists one breakfast store, but no breakfast-out habit has been measured, so ramp-up is likely to be slow ASSUMPTION [A3 §3.1; A4 §3.1].
  4. Site logic. Year-round volume sits near Yerevan on the Yerevan-bound side. Interchange parcels (Abovyan km 15.7–16.7, Charentsavan 31.1, Hrazdan 39.3 and 47.4) serve both directions [B1 §4], and a CPS or independent CNG forecourt beyond the km 10–13 cluster inherits stopping traffic [A3 §4].
Decision. Replace "premium American-style drive-thru on the M4" with a testable concept: a fast, car-first coffee-and-hot-food unit with an Armenian menu at affordable-premium prices, on a Yerevan exit corridor, an interchange or a fuel forecourt. Treat any revenue case with an average order above about 2,000 AMD ($5.53) or more than about 150 transactions a day as a stretch case until field counts support it [A4 §4; A2 §4; C6 §4]. Sign no land or lease before the checks below.

What we know vs what we estimate

  • Known: 41.2% of households own a car; Coffee House runs the only known drive-thru, on the M4, at mass prices, with long summer waits; mass to-go coffee costs 800–1,000 AMD and premium chains stop at about 1,950; Max Oil and Flash are adding cafés along the corridor; the last official M4 count is from 2008; most "tourists" are long-stay visitors.
  • Estimated or assumed: 2025 traffic by segment; seasonality of 1.5× near Yerevan and 2.3–2.6× beyond Hrazdan; capture of 0.3–1.3% and 80–150 transactions a day; an average order of 1,500–1,900 AMD with food; segment sizes; whether people will use a lane for breakfast; how fast incumbents copy.

Field checks before any land commitment

  1. Traffic counts. Request 2019–2026 M4 counter data from the Road Department Fund and MTAI, and Road Police camera counts. Run 7-day classified video counts at six screenlines and the shortlisted parcel in summer and winter, with plate matching and stop-rate counts at existing stops: about 6.5–12.7 M AMD ($18–35k), or 1.4–2.9 M AMD ($4–8k) for a single-parcel pre-screen ASSUMPTION [B1 §5].
  2. Observe the Coffee House Drive-Thru on three weekdays and two weekend days, three dayparts each, summer and winter: cars per hour, order-to-pickup time, queue length, direction of travel [A2 §5].
  3. Intercept and household survey of at least 600 people in Yerevan, Kotayk and Gegharkunik, with quotas by car ownership and age: coffee frequency, breakfast out, drive-thru use, acceptable wait and a price ladder at 1,000 / 1,500 / 2,000 / 2,500 AMD; plus 150–200 intercepts per roadside stop per season [A1 §5; A4 §5; B1 §5].
  4. Mystery shopping at Max Cafe km 12.2 (both sides), Flash and CPS in Sevan, Abovyan and Hrazdan, and Gas #1 at km 51: coffee prices, cup sizes, machine type, food, toilets and CNG dwell time. Photograph menu boards at 15 brands in store and compare with Glovo [A3 §5; A2 §5].
  5. News sweep of 2018–2026 drive-thru openings and closures before treating "only one" as final [A3 §5].
