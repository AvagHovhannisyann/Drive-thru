Phase 5 · Architecture & 3D

Architecture and 3D prototype

The reference prototype is a 171 m² tuff-clad pavilion on a 3,256 m² plot. Two order lanes merge into one pickup lane that wraps the back of the building, giving about 12–13 cars of stacking before the queue reaches the entry throat. Drawings, renders and the interactive model all come from one parametric source, so dimensions agree across every view. It is the format for a strong standalone site once the brand is proven. The recommended first unit is CHAMPA Compact, a 90 m² version that shares a fuel-station forecourt, because the prototype as drawn needs longer highway lanes than its plot allows and may trigger an environmental assessment.

Design concept

  • Traffic first. The highway queue is the biggest safety and permitting risk. The lane runs away from the road: entry from a deceleration lane at the south-east, stacking up the east side, pickup along the north façade, exit down the west side to an acceleration lane. A spill-back onto the carriageway would need more than 12 cars waiting at once.
  • A roof that reads at 90 km/h. A hyperbolic-paraboloid roof (lowest corner 4.15 m, highest 7.30 m) sweeps up towards the approach, with an oak-batten soffit and a clerestory. An 11 m tuff tower carries the lit symbol; the 8 m pylon stands at the entry.
  • Armenian materials, modern detailing. Sawn volcanic tuff (apricot/rose blend, open joints), dark-bronze aluminium frames, low-iron solar-control glazing, basalt pavers on the plaza.
  • Hybrid by design. The same building serves the drive-thru lane, walk-up customers, a 12-seat café, a 62 m² terrace and two accessible unisex restrooms with baby change. Restrooms are deliberate: on intercity roads the fuel stations own the stop because they offer toilets and parking [A3 §4].

C6's queueing benchmark is 10–12 stacked cars for a window serving at least 60 cars per hour [C6 §1 #5]; the operations plan finds that summer peaks need the windows to run at 80–90 cars per hour for this stack to hold [STRATEGY:operations].

Permitting check: three defects in the prototype as drawn.
  1. Speed-change lanes are too short. The layout carries an 86 m deceleration lane and a 121 m acceleration lane. The Armenian road norm ՀՀՇՆ 32-01-2022 requires 90–110 m at full width plus an 80 m taper to decelerate (170–190 m in total) and 140–230 m plus an 80 m taper to accelerate (220–310 m) on any connection to an IA–IC road carrying 50 or more vehicles a day VERIFIED [C5 §1 #3]. Both lanes together are about 1,155–1,500 m² of heavy-duty pavement, roughly 27–43 M AMD ($75–119k) ESTIMATE, and they run well beyond a 74 m frontage, so they need the road owner's land and consent.
  2. The paving can trigger an environmental impact assessment. Landscaping or improvement works with 1,000 m² or more of impervious surface fall in EIA Category B, which adds a public hearing and a community-council vote that can veto the project VERIFIED [C5 §1 #4]. The prototype has about 1,870 m² of hard surface excluding the building; permeable paving on parking and the plaza would bring it to about 970 m² ESTIMATE [STRATEGY:operations].
  3. The 8 m pylon may count as advertising, which brings a minimum distance from junctions and an annual road-side advertising tax [STRATEGY:operations; C4].
These findings favour sites that already have a compliant access, such as an existing fuel-station forecourt or an urban arterial, over a new connection to a 90 km/h highway.
Both-direction service is a site decision, not a design feature. On a divided road, a right-in/right-out pad only serves one carriageway. Serving both directions needs a grade-separated interchange, a signalised junction or a legal U-turn nearby. That is why the location study (Chapter 3) scores access geometry separately.

CHAMPA Compact: the first-unit format

The financial and permitting evidence points to a smaller first unit than the reference prototype (Chapters 6–7). CHAMPA Compact keeps the design language (tuff, oak soffit, a small hyperbolic-paraboloid roof, the beacon tower) on a 12.0 × 7.5 m (90 m²) pavilion. C1 sizes a 90 m² lean building at about 92 M AMD all-in on a greenfield plot and about 70.5 M AMD ($195k) when co-located on a fuel-station forecourt, excluding equipment and land ESTIMATE [C1 §3 summary].

  • One drive-thru lane (one order point, one pickup window) wrapping the pavilion, with about 7 cars of stacking from window to forecourt entry. That is below the 10–12-car design stack for standalone pads, which is acceptable only because the host forecourt absorbs overflow away from the highway.
  • Walk-up window and a three-table terrace under the roof overhang; no indoor seating.
  • Restrooms, fuel, parking aisles and the highway connection are shared with the host under the lease, which removes the access lanes, most paving and the EIA paving trigger from the CHAMPA budget. Whether adding a tenant changes the host's existing access consent must be confirmed with the road owner ASSUMPTION.

Options and adaptation

  • Rooftop PV. The upper roof planes can take about 99 modules (≈40 kWp at 400 W per module) ESTIMATE. Armenian net metering is available up to 150 kW per connection REPORTED [C1]. Shown in the renders, not included in base capex.
  • Compact variant (see below). The lean first-unit format: a 90 m² pavilion on a leased corner of a host fuel-station forecourt, sharing its access, restrooms and utilities.
  • Climate. Winter operation needs snow storage on the plot edges, heated window sills and air curtains at the windows; summer needs shade on the stacking lanes. Both are cheaper to plan now than to retrofit.
