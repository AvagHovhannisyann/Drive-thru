# A1. Armenian macroeconomy, purchasing power and payment behaviour

Prepared for the premium drive-thru coffee & food feasibility study (the M4 Yerevan–Sevan concept and its alternatives).
Research date: 2026-10-10. All figures carry a status tag:

- **VERIFIED**: I opened the primary or official source myself (URL given).
- **REPORTED**: a credible secondary source. "(snippet)" means I only saw it in a search-result snippet and did not open the page.
- **ESTIMATE**: my own calculation, with the formula shown.
- **ASSUMPTION**: a judgment call, with the basis explained.

Source numbers in [brackets] refer to Section 6.

## 1. Executive summary

1. **Growth is strong, but forecasts expect it to slow.** Real GDP grew **+7.1% in 2025** (Armstat revised figure; +7.2% preliminary). Nominal GDP was **AMD 11.34 trn**. The economic activity index rose **+7.3% y/y in Jan–Aug 2026**. Forecasts for 2026 cluster at **5.3–5.7%**: IMF WEO 5.3%, IMF SBA review "around 5¼%", World Bank 5.6% (latest) and CBA 5.3–5.7%. GDP per capita was **≈$9.5k in 2025**, and the IMF projects **$10.4k nominal / $27.0k PPP for 2026** [VERIFIED: 3, 13, 6].
2. **Local purchasing power is low compared with an American-style ticket.**
   - The average gross wage was **AMD 312,486/month in Jan–Aug 2026** (≈$863). In Yerevan it was **AMD 357,583** in Q2 2026.
   - Along the M4 corridor it is much lower: **Kotayk AMD 208,210** (58% of Yerevan) and **Gegharkunik AMD 158,949** (44%).
   - Accommodation & food workers earned **AMD 160,253** (Jan–Aug 2026). The minimum wage is still **AMD 75,000**, and the average pension is **AMD 49,451** [VERIFIED: 4, 5, 3; minimum wage REPORTED: 33].
3. **The official exchange rate for the model is AMD 361.93 per USD.** This is the CBA rate set on **Fri 9 Oct 2026**, which also applies on Sat 10 Oct.
   - 12-month range: **361.81 (5 Oct 2026) to 383.05 (24 Oct 2025)**; 12-month average 373.39.
   - The dram has strengthened **5.4% y/y** against the USD. EUR = 405.58 and RUB = 4.2677 [VERIFIED: 1].
   - Recommendation: use 362 as the base rate and stress-test 345–390.
4. **Inflation is above target again and policy has turned tighter.**
   - CPI was **4.3% y/y in Sept 2026**; food was **6.2%** and restaurants & hotels **5.4%**. Inflation has been above the 3% ±1 target band since Feb 2026.
   - The CBA **raised its policy rate to 6.75%** on 15 Sep 2026, the first increase in over three years.
   - Petrol CPI was **+11.7% y/y** in Aug 2026 and diesel **+25.0%**. AI-95 sells at about **AMD 630/l**, a record high [VERIFIED: 7, 17; REPORTED: 8, 31].
5. **A 1,500–2,500 AMD drink is priced like a drink in the US.**
   - In dollars it is **$4.14–6.91**, which in PPP terms is like paying about $14 in the US for a 2,000-AMD drink.
   - At the average net wage, one 2,000-AMD drink costs **about 1.5 hours of work**; for a food-service worker it costs about 2.9 hours.
   - A habit of eight drinks a month would take **≥11.6% of per-capita income for 90% of the population** (deciles I–IX). Only decile X comes in under 10% [ESTIMATE from 9].
6. **Household survey data show very little spending on eating out.**
   - The average urban resident spends **AMD 2,293/month** on food away from home, which is **3.5%** of consumption. That is about one premium coffee a month. Nationally the figure is 1,709 AMD (2.8%).
   - Food takes **43.5%** of consumption on average and **52.5%** in the poorest decile [VERIFIED: 9, ILCS 2024].
   - The household survey almost certainly under-records spending at the top. National catering turnover works out to about **AMD 8,400 per capita per month** [ESTIMATE from 10].
7. **Poverty is 21.7%** (2024). It is **16.9% in Yerevan, 24.8% in Kotayk and 27.6% in Gegharkunik**. Income Gini is 0.339 and the top/bottom quintile income ratio is 5.5×. The towns around the M4 are therefore poorer than Yerevan, so a highway site would depend on **Yerevan-origin leisure traffic and tourists**, not on local demand [VERIFIED: 9].
8. **Card payments are growing quickly, and the typical card purchase is now coffee-sized.**
   - There are **5.47 m cards** (1.76 per resident), and **87% of them are NFC-enabled**.
   - Physical-POS purchases grew **+41% in value and +66% in count** in H1 2026 y/y. The **average POS ticket fell to AMD 3,473** in Q2 2026, from 6,654 in Q1 2024.
   - Apple Pay works with 17 Armenian issuers, including Telcell Wallet, and Google Pay is live.
   - The CBA capped acquiring fees from 1 Mar 2026 at **0.5% for ArCa and 0.9% for Visa/Mastercard**, for businesses with turnover of AMD 150 m or less.
   - Even so, **cash withdrawals were still about 44% of card turnover** in H1 2026 [VERIFIED: 19–21, 26; REPORTED: 23, 25].
9. **Car ownership is modest, which limits who can use a drive-thru.**
   - **41.2% of households own a car** (ILCS 2024) [VERIFIED: 9].
   - About **954 k vehicles** of all types were registered as of 1 Sep 2024, which is about **310 per 1,000 residents**; Yerevan had 343 k [REPORTED: 32; ESTIMATE].
   - No official passenger-car count for 2025–26 was found.
10. **Eating-out demand grew very fast after 2022, partly because of foreigners.**
    - Food & beverage services grew in real terms by **+18.9% (2022), +27.1% (2023) and +10.9% (2024)**. Accommodation & food services grew **+13.9% in Jan–Aug 2026**.
    - Foreign cards spent **AMD 164.6 bn at Armenian POS terminals in 2025** [VERIFIED: 10, 6, 21].
11. **Relocants: there is a new inflow in 2026, but it depends on geopolitics.**
    - There is no official figure for how many relocants remain.
    - Border records show net entries of Russian citizens of **+65,750 in Jan–Aug 2026**, against +15,780 in Jan–Aug 2025 and +18,797 in Jan–Aug 2024. The World Bank describes a "new influx of Russians" driving Yerevan property prices up (city-centre prices **+12.7% y/y** in July 2026).
    - This is upside for Western-style cafés in **Yerevan**. It is not stable demand: it depends on Russian politics and on Armenia–Russia relations, which worsened in 2026 [VERIFIED: 6, 12, 15].
12. **2026 brought several macro shocks.**
    - Russia banned Armenian agri-food, fish and some beverage imports between April and June 2026. The EAEU has pressed Armenia to hold a referendum.
    - A Middle East war pushed up energy and logistics costs.
    - On the positive side, Turkey lifted its direct-trade restrictions on 13 May 2026, and the June 2026 election returned the incumbent government [VERIFIED: 15, 14; REPORTED: 36].

## 4. Implications for the drive-thru concept (critical)

**What supports a premium coffee and food concept**

1. **Macro momentum.** Growth of 5–7% a year, real wage growth, retail trade +7% and catering volume +14% in 2026 all point to a growing pool of discretionary spending. Food & beverage services roughly doubled in nominal terms between 2021 and 2024.
2. **Payments are ready.** Card use is everywhere: 87% of cards are NFC-enabled, Apple Pay and Google Pay work, QR wallets are widely accepted, and the falling POS ticket shows people paying by card for small amounts. A cashless or mostly-cashless, app-pre-order drive-thru is operationally feasible. Merchant fee caps (0.5–0.9%) help margins if the business qualifies.
3. **Smartphones and mobile internet are near-universal** among working-age urban adults, so app ordering and loyalty programmes can be used.
4. **A strong dram lowers imported input costs** (green coffee, machines) in AMD terms.

**Disconfirming evidence and risks**

1. **The price point does not fit local incomes.**
   - At AMD 1,500–2,500, a drink costs **$4–7**, which is US-level, in a country where the average net wage is about AMD 231 k (≈$640) and the median household income is far lower.
   - Habitual purchase realistically comes only from the **top 10–20%** of the income distribution, plus expats, relocants and tourists.
   - The household survey shows the average urban resident spends about **AMD 2,300/month on all food away from home**.
2. **The M4 catchment is poorer than Yerevan.**
   - Kotayk and Gegharkunik wages are **58% and 44% of Yerevan's**, and their poverty rates (24.8% and 27.6%) are well above Yerevan's 16.9%.
   - Only about 146 k people live in the corridor towns. A highway site therefore depends on **discretionary leisure trips from Yerevan** (Sevan in summer, Tsaghkadzor in winter, Dilijan) and on tourists. That means **strong seasonality and weekday weakness** [ASSUMPTION; to be tested with traffic counts in another workstream].
3. **The car-owning base is modest.**
   - **41% of households own a car** and there are about 310 vehicles of all types per 1,000 residents.
   - Taxis (28% of passenger trips) and walking dominate in Yerevan. In US markets drive-thru penetration rests on near-universal car ownership; that is not the case here.
4. **Fuel prices are at records** (AI-95 AMD 630, petrol +11.7% y/y, diesel +25%) and inflation expectations are rising, which may squeeze leisure driving and discretionary spending in 2026–27.
5. **Cost inflation outpaces headline inflation.** Food CPI is 6–9%, the retail coffee price rose +14.7% in 2025, wages are growing about 5.6%, and Yerevan catering CPI is +6%. Margins will be squeezed unless prices rise, and price rises hurt affordability.
6. **Relocant- and tourist-driven demand is volatile.** It depends on Russian politics and on Armenia–Russia relations, which worsened in 2026. In a downside scenario (Russia–Armenia rupture, end of inflows), the dram could weaken by 5–8% and Yerevan's premium-café demand could fall.
7. **Wage data understate pay in food service.** The official sector wage of AMD 160 k is a floor, so actual labour costs for reliable, English- or Russian-speaking staff will be higher [ASSUMPTION].

**What this means for the alternatives [ASSUMPTION; to be integrated with traffic, competition and real-estate workstreams]**

- **Urban and suburban Yerevan sites** are better supported by purchasing-power data: higher wages, lower poverty, and the relocant, expat and tourist base. The north-eastern exit districts toward the M4 (Nor Nork, Avan, Kanaker-Zeytun, 272.6 k residents) combine commuter, urban and outbound leisure traffic.
- **Fuel-station hybrids** benefit from existing car flow, an established cashless habit at stations, and a shared rent burden. They suit a broader, mid-priced menu.
- **Menu and pricing:** use a **two-tier price architecture**. Offer a local-affordable entry tier (filter or Armenian-style coffee, a basic latte) alongside premium signature drinks, and add food to lift the ticket. The average POS ticket of AMD 3,473 suggests a coffee + snack basket of AMD 2,500–3,500 is in line with how people already pay by card [ESTIMATE/ASSUMPTION].
