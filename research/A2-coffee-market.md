## 1. Executive summary

1. **A local market leader already runs a drive-thru on the M4 Yerevan–Sevan corridor. This is the single most important finding for the concept.** Coffee House Company, Armenia's largest takeaway coffee chain, lists a "Drive-Thru" branch at coordinates 40.2593 N, 44.5976 E. OpenStreetMap places that point on the *Yerevan–Dilijan Highway* (M4) at Ptghni/Balahovit, Kotayk, about 11.6 km in a straight line from Republic Square. It is open 08:30–24:00 daily, and Yandex rates it 4.9★ from 123 ratings. Its prices are mass-market: cappuccino 800–1,200 AMD, iced latte 900 AMD. (VERIFIED company site [S9]; REPORTED Yandex [S10]; ESTIMATE for the distance.) The "first American-style drive-thru on the M4" story is therefore **not available**. A newcomer has to win on speed, quality, food and experience against a well-known brand selling at roughly 1,000 AMD.
2. **Coffee House dominates the to-go segment.** It claims 57 branches "across Armenia and beyond", 250+ employees, 300,000+ regular customers and 5M+ coffees made. Its branch page lists 52 points: 42 in Yerevan, plus Zvartnots airport, Abovyan, Tsaghkadzor, Gyumri, Vanadzor, Ejmiatsin and Armavir. On Glovo (Oct 2026) it charges americano 800, cappuccino or latte 1,000, flat white 1,100 and iced latte 1,000 AMD. In September 2025 it opened a premium specialty sub-brand, FEIN, with an in-house Probat roaster. (VERIFIED [S8][S9]; REPORTED [S11][S26].)
3. **The market is price-sensitive.** Average gross monthly wage was 314,196 AMD in Aug 2026 (≈$868; VERIFIED Armstat [S1]). A 1,950 AMD Coffeeshop Company cappuccino therefore costs about 1.1 hours of average gross pay; a Coffee House cappuccino costs about 0.55 hours (ESTIMATE). Numbeo's crowdsourced average cappuccino in Yerevan is 1,342 AMD, with a range of 800–2,000 AMD (REPORTED, updated 3 Oct 2026 [S13]).
4. **Price ladder for a regular cappuccino or latte (AMD):**
   - Mass/kiosk: 800–1,000 (Coffee House).
   - Mid: about 1,300–1,400 (Cofix in-store estimate; Numbeo average).
   - Upper-mid/premium chain: 1,600–1,950 (Surf Coffee 1,600–1,800 for 300 ml; Coffeeshop Company 1,950).
   - Crowdsourced ceiling: about 2,000.

   We put **"affordable premium" at 1,400–1,600 AMD for a 350 ml cappuccino or latte and 1,600–1,800 AMD for a 400 ml iced latte**. That is about 40–60% above the mass leader and about 15–25% below the premium chains. (ESTIMATE/ASSUMPTION; see §3.6.)
5. **Imports show a market that is flat in volume but much more expensive.** HS 0901 imports were 12,691 t / $24.6M in 2019 and 14,074 t / $79.6M in 2025: volume +11%, value +224%. The green-coffee unit value nearly tripled, from $1.82/kg to $5.31/kg. Coffee CPI rose 9.5% in 2025 against 3.3% headline CPI. (VERIFIED UN Comtrade, Armenia-reported [S2]; REPORTED Aysor citing Armstat [S14].) Net of exports (mostly re-exports and roasted coffee to Russia), apparent consumption was flat to slightly down: about 12.4k t green-bean equivalent (GBE) in 2019 and about 11.6k t in 2025 (ESTIMATE).
6. **Per-capita consumption is about 3.8–4.8 kg GBE per year (2019–2025, our estimate from Comtrade net trade).** That is well below the 8.25 kg (2023) that Helgi Library attributes to FAOSTAT. We trust our figure more because it nets out large post-2022 re-exports. The two sources conflict, and our figure is the conservative one. (ESTIMATE; REPORTED [S15].)
7. **Armenian (soorj/jezve) coffee still dominates by volume.**
   - Indonesia and Vietnam, both robusta-heavy origins, supplied **89% of green imports by weight in 2025**.
   - Arabica-type origins (Ethiopia, Brazil, Colombia, Central America, Kenya and others) supplied about 6.9%.
   - Roasted (espresso-type) imports were only 871 t, about 6% of the 0901 volume.

   This is VERIFIED Comtrade data, used here as a volume proxy (ESTIMATE). We found **no survey that measures the soorj vs espresso split**; this is a key gap.
8. **Specialty and third-wave coffee is real but small and confined to central Yerevan.** wte.am lists 22 specialty coffee shops. Lumen (two locations, own Giesen roaster) is reported at #61 in "Europe's 100 Best Coffee Shops 2026". Roasters include Lumen, 202°F, AfroLab, Nero Bean, Hayk, FEIN, DOC Therapy and The Coffee 23.5. Imports from micro-lot origins (Kenya, Rwanda, Guatemala, Costa Rica, Honduras and similar) were only about 54 t in 2025. (VERIFIED/REPORTED [S2][S17][S18][S19].) None of these venues sits on a highway.
9. **The international chains Americans would recognise are absent:**
   - **Starbucks:** none in Armenia. It held exploratory talks with Armenia's Economy Minister in Oct 2025 (VERIFIED Arka/Hetq [S5][S6]) and opened its first Georgian store, through Alshaya, in July 2026 (REPORTED [S7]).
   - **Stars Coffee** (the Russian successor to Starbucks): named Armenia as its first foreign market in 2022, but we found no evidence that it opened (REPORTED [S27]).
   - **Costa, Tim Hortons, Dunkin', Coffee Like, Double B, Skuratov:** no evidence of Armenian outlets.
   - **Foreign or relocated brands that are present:** Cofix (Israel; master franchise held by Galaxy Group since Feb 2024), Surf Coffee (Russia; 3 Yerevan stores), Coffeeshop Company (Austria; 3 branches), Segafredo and Paul. (§3.4.)
10. **Starbucks entering Armenia is a 12–36-month competitive risk to an "American-style" positioning.** The Georgia launch shows that Alshaya has started covering the Caucasus (ASSUMPTION on timing).
11. **Franchise economics in Armenia are not public.** The closest benchmark is Coffee Like (Russia). The franchisor says its drive-through kiosk ("avtopavilion") averages about 1.22M RUB/month in revenue (≈5.2M AMD), with an average check of 393 RUB (≈1,670 AMD), about 103 checks/day, and investment from 2.0M RUB (≈8.5M AMD). These are marketing claims from Russia (REPORTED [S24]). Our rough estimate puts Coffee House's own average throughput at only about 40–70 cups per branch per day (ESTIMATE, low confidence).

**Apparent consumption and per capita (ESTIMATE)**

Formula, in green-bean equivalent (GBE):

> GBE = (green imports − green exports) + 1.19 × (roasted imports − roasted exports) + 2.6 × instant extract imports (210111) − 2.6 × 0.5 × HS 2101 exports

- The 1.19 (roasted) and 2.6 (soluble) factors are the standard ICO conversion factors. Applying them here is an ASSUMPTION.
- 2101 exports are treated as 50% extract because the split is unknown (ASSUMPTION).
- 3-in-1 preparations (210112) are excluded because they are mostly sugar and creamer.
- Population is from the World Bank [S3].

**How to read this**

- **Volume is not growing.** Net coffee use was about 11–14k t GBE in every year. The 2021–23 bump in imports came with a jump in re-exports to Russia after 2022.
- **Triangulation.** A 2019 interview with the coffee company Kaffa said Armenia sells about 1,000 t of coffee a month (≈12,000 t/yr) [S28]. That matches the 2019 net import figure of 11.7k t.
- **Conflict with FAOSTAT.** Helgi Library reports FAOSTAT per-capita consumption of 8.25 kg in 2023 (+23.9% y/y) [S15]. Our net figure for the same year is 4.6 kg. The FAOSTAT method was not visible (paywalled Excel). We suspect it does not net out the re-exports that surged after 2022. **For modelling, use 3.8–4.6 kg GBE per capita.**
- **Cost inflation is the real story.** The import bill (0901 + 2101) rose from $29.7M in 2019 to $96.7M in 2025. Net of exports it was about $57.3M in 2025 (ESTIMATE). Green unit cost rose 192%. A 2019 Hetq analysis already noted that Armenia exports coffee at about twice its import price per kg ($4 vs $2/kg, 2015–19) [S29]. That reflects roasting value-add for the Russian and Georgian markets.
- **Instant coffee is a fast-growing mass segment.** Instant extract (210111) imports rose from 356 t to 941 t between 2019 and 2025 (+164%). Coffee House, the leading kiosk chain, sells "Nescafe", 3-in-1 and "Dolce Gusto mix" drinks at 500–650 AMD alongside espresso drinks [S11]. Many consumers buy sweet, milky, cheap coffee.

### 3.2 Soorj vs espresso: what the evidence supports

- **Cultural baseline:** Coffee is "a staple of every meal" in Armenia and is usually brewed unfiltered in a jezve (Sprudge, Apr 2019 [S30]; Yandex Eats "Open Kitchen", Jul 2025 [S18]). In 2019, Kaffa said Armenians prefer robusta to arabica, partly because it is cheaper [S28].
- **Trade-mix proxy for 2025** (VERIFIED data, ESTIMATE interpretation):
  - About 89% of green imports come from robusta-dominant origins (Indonesia, Vietnam). Local roasters such as Royal Armenia, which processes about 3,000 t/yr [S16], grind most of this for jezve coffee.
  - Arabica-type origins make up about 6.9% of green volume (≈907 t).
  - European roasted coffee (Italy, Belgium, Germany, France, Switzerland, Spain) was about 353 t, roughly the espresso-blend and capsule segment.
  - Our order-of-magnitude reading is that espresso-type coffee is **about 10–15% of national coffee volume**, with soorj/jezve and instant making up the rest. This is an ASSUMPTION built on the import mix. It cannot be validated without a consumer survey, and espresso blends also contain robusta.
- **Direction of travel:**
  - Coffee House alone grew from about 30+ to 40+ to 57 branches of takeaway espresso drinks (see §3.5). It reports 5M+ cups made since 2015 [S8].
  - Roasted espresso-type imports from Italy, Belgium and Germany cost $16–17/kg.
  - Specialty roasters multiplied (see §3.3).

  Out-of-home espresso is clearly growing faster than total coffee volume, which is flat. That implies soorj/at-home volume is flat or declining. This is an ESTIMATE that needs a survey to confirm.
- **No published survey was found** in English, Armenian or Russian searches that quantifies the soorj/espresso split or the at-home/out-of-home split. See §5 for how to close this gap.

**Bean cost check (ESTIMATE).** An 18 g double shot of Italian roasted coffee at the 2025 import unit value ($16.36/kg CIF) costs about 107 AMD, or about 128 AMD with 20% VAT. The 192% rise in green-coffee unit value since 2019 matters more for the mass segment than for a premium price point.

## 4. Implications for the drive-thru concept (critical)

1. **The M4 drive-thru idea is already being tested by the strongest local operator.** Coffee House runs a 16-hour, highly rated drive-thru on M4 at mass prices, plus branches in Abovyan city and Tsaghkadzor along the Sevan route. A premium American-style entrant would face direct substitution at about half its price point. Before committing, the study should:
   - Observe this site in the field: cars/hour by daypart, weekday vs weekend, summer vs winter, queue length, and the share of Sevan-bound traffic.
   - Treat it as the live benchmark for demand.

   Its 4.9★ rating shows customers accept the format. The 20–30-minute wait complaints show an **opening on speed and consistency**, not on concept novelty.
2. **Price sensitivity limits a Starbucks-level ticket.** A 1,950 AMD cappuccino is about 1.1 hours of average gross pay. Mass to-go coffee in Armenia clears at 800–1,000 AMD, and even the premium chains stop at about 1,950. A US-style $5–6 (1,800–2,200 AMD) drink is only viable for large sizes, signature iced drinks or tourist/Sevan-weekend demand. Base-case modelling should use an average beverage ticket of about 1,300–1,500 AMD, not 1,800+ (ASSUMPTION).
3. **The core coffee habit (jezve/soorj, robusta, home and office) is not what a drive-thru sells.** About 89% of green coffee imports are robusta-origin volume for jezve coffee. Total volume is flat at about 11.6–12.4k t GBE. Growth comes from moving into to-go espresso drinks, much of it sweet and milky (frappés, Kinder/Nutella lattes, instant mixes). The menu should lean into iced, sweet and flavoured drinks and fast food items, not only "authentic" espresso. Adding a quality jezve or "Armenian coffee to go" SKU is worth testing as a local differentiator (ASSUMPTION).
4. **Cost inflation is structural.** Green coffee cost per kg nearly tripled from 2019 to 2025, and coffee CPI rose 9.5% in 2025. The model should include coffee COGS escalation of +5–10% per year in a downside case and should not assume the 2025 FX rate (AMD is strong at about 362/USD) persists (ASSUMPTION).
5. **Starbucks entry risk is now concrete.** The Oct 2025 ministerial talks and the July 2026 Georgia launch mean an "American-style" brand promise could be overtaken by the original within the model horizon. A US-themed concept without a defensible operations edge (speed, drive-thru lanes, food) is exposed. On the other hand, Starbucks would most likely open in Yerevan malls and the centre first, not on highways (ASSUMPTION).
6. **Food attach matters, and Armenian chains already sell food.**
   - Coffeeshop Company and Jazzve are café-restaurants with sandwiches at 2,400–4,400 AMD.
   - Coffee House sells mostly sweets.
   - A drive-thru with good hot savoury food (breakfast sandwiches, wraps, lahmajo or khachapuri-style items) could raise the ticket to about 3,500–4,000 AMD and differentiate it from Coffee House.
7. **Specialty scale is small and urban.** Specialty growth (22 listed shops, Lumen in a European top-100) is concentrated in Kentron. It should not be used as evidence of demand on the M4. On the corridor, a "premium" claim must be expressed through product consistency and speed, not third-wave cues.
8. **Alternative formats look at least as attractive and should be modelled alongside M4:**
   - Urban or suburban drive-thru or walk-up kiosks near Yerevan exits, business parks and residential districts (Coffee House's urban network shows to-go demand density).
   - Fuel-station hybrids (an existing traffic stop, with lower land and permit risk).
   - A broader food menu.
   - The Coffee Like benchmark (about 103 checks/day for drive-through pavilions in Russia) against our rough Coffee House estimate (about 40–70 cups per branch per day) suggests **base-case volumes of 80–150 transactions/day for a well-sited unit** (ASSUMPTION). Anything above that needs field traffic evidence.
