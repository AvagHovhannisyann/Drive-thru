# C1 — Construction, site-development and utility-connection costs (Armenia, 2025–2026)

**Scope:** Single-storey commercial drive-thru building of about 90–180 m² on a 2,000–3,500 m² plot. Reference location is the M4 Yerevan–Sevan corridor (Kotayk / Gegharkunik marzes), but the location is not fixed.
**Prepared:** 2026-10-10. **Currency:** AMD; USD at **362 AMD/USD** (CBA official rate on 9 Oct 2026: 361.93), unless stated otherwise.
**Status tags:** VERIFIED = primary or official source I opened; REPORTED = credible secondary source (news, supplier list price, listing); ESTIMATE = my calculation, with inputs shown; ASSUMPTION = judgment, with the basis explained.
**Important caveat:** No supplier or contractor quotations were obtained. Every supplier price below is an online list price, and negotiated prices for a project will differ. Kitchen and coffee equipment, land, land-use change and pre-opening costs are **excluded**; other workstreams cover them.

---

## 1. Executive summary

1. **The three build options come out at roughly $325k, $545k and $1.06M (all-in, excl. land and equipment, greenfield M4 site with norm-compliant access lanes).** These are ESTIMATES.
   - Lean modular: **≈ AMD 118M (≈ $327k)**; range AMD 116–126M ($320–347k) depending on the access-lane length. With an existing compliant access instead of new lanes: ≈ AMD 92M ($255k). Co-located on a fuel-station forecourt: ≈ AMD 70.5M ($195k).
   - Mid-range steel-frame: **≈ AMD 197M (≈ $545k)**; range AMD 195–205M ($538–566k).
   - Flagship architect-designed: **≈ AMD 382M (≈ $1.06M)**; range AMD 379–386M ($1.05–1.07M).
   - *Fact-check correction: the earlier totals (AMD 92M / 183M / 376M) carried M4 access works of AMD 7–28M. Road norm ՀՀՇՆ 32-01-2022 Table 31 requires full speed-change lanes costing ≈ AMD 27–36M per greenfield M4 site (§3.4). The totals above also add the EIA Category B state duty (AMD 315k; see §4 item 5).*
   - All three include VAT, soft costs and a 10–12% contingency. **Use these gross, VAT-inclusive totals as the base case.** *Fact-check correction: the earlier text said net cash cost is "about 1/6 lower" for a VAT-registered company. Sibling C4-tax recommends catering turnover tax for a single unit, under which capex VAT cannot be recovered; registering for VAT during construction and then switching to TT triggers a clawback of 20% of net book value (Tax Code Art. 73(14)), worth only ≈ AMD 5M of timing benefit. The ÷1.2 figure is kept only as a flagship / VAT-regime sensitivity, and even then overstates recovery (duties carry no VAT; many small contractors issue no VAT invoices).*
   - Uncertainty is about **±25–35%**, because most building-system, signage and technology lines have no Armenian quote yet.
2. **Site works, not the building, drive the budget.** Paving, drainage, lighting, landscaping and M4 access lanes are **32–47% of total cost** (47% lean, 40% mid, 32% flagship), against **20–24% for the building**. The largest single site item is the highway access works (deceleration and acceleration lanes). Armenian road norm ՀՀՇՆ 32-01-2022 Table 31 requires a 140–230 m acceleration lane, a 90–110 m deceleration lane and an 80 m taper on each for any drive to a roadside building on an IA/IB/IC/II road with ≥ 50 PCU/day. That is ≈ 1,155–1,500 m² of heavy-duty pavement, or **≈ AMD 27–36M per greenfield M4 site** (ESTIMATE; the norm is VERIFIED by the fact-check). I carry AMD 29.2M (lean, mid) and 32.6M (flagship) at the middle column of Table 31. *Fact-check correction (WRONG → fixed): the earlier text said the standard had not been found and carried AMD 7–28M as an ASSUMPTION, which put shares at 31–37% site works and 21–27% building. The lean ~300 m² taper does not comply with the norm. Only a site with an existing access, or with access from a local road or interchange, avoids this cost.*
3. **Building cost per m² (ESTIMATE, Q4-2026, incl. VAT):**
   - Economy/modular: ≈ **AMD 200–300k/m² ($550–830)**.
   - Standard steel-frame + sandwich panel: ≈ **AMD 250–350k ($690–970)**. My bottom-up mid-range case gives AMD 277k ($766).
   - Premium architect-designed: ≈ **AMD 450–650k ($1,240–1,800)**.
   - These sit inside the only published Armenian benchmark: $500–800/m² standard and $1,200–2,000+ premium (residential, 2024), with commercial running 20–30% higher (REPORTED, low confidence).
   - **Modular is not materially cheaper per m² in Armenia**, because local site labour is cheap and imports add freight plus a 5% EAEU duty. Its advantages are speed and the option to relocate.
4. **Construction prices in AMD are flat; prices in USD are rising.**
   - The official construction price index (Ministry of Finance, published by Armstat, VERIFIED) fell **−4.3% Dec/Dec in 2025** (annual average −6.3%) and rose only **+1.5% y/y in Aug 2026**.
   - Over the same year the AMD appreciated ~4.6% against the USD, and CPI is +4.4% y/y.
   - Construction volume is booming: **+24.7% nominal in Jan–Aug 2026**, and housing completions rose 70% in 2024. That points to contractor-capacity and labour tightness, which the official index may understate.
   - Budget **4–6% p.a. escalation in AMD** (ASSUMPTION).
5. **The electricity connection fee is cheap, but connection risk is high.**
   - Since **27 Apr 2026** the PSRC standard fee for a 0.4 kV commercial connection is **AMD 229,000 + 9,000/kVA in the regions** (16,000/kVA in Yerevan). That is **≈ AMD 1.13M ($3.1k) for 100 kVA**, down from ≈ AMD 2.3M (REPORTED).
   - The standard fee covers sites inside settlement boundaries, or up to 200 m from the grid connection point outside them. A highway plot outside a settlement pays extra for line length and, if needed, a 10/0.4 kV substation. I found no published unit costs for this, so it is the key utility risk (ASSUMPTION AMD 2–12M contingency; potentially more).
   - The distributor ENA has been under a state temporary manager since Jul 2025. Its licence was revoked by the PSRC on 17 Nov 2025, with **connection-timeline violations** among the cited grounds (REPORTED).
   - Statutory connection times: **60 days at 0.4 kV, 90 days at 6/10 kV**. Plan for longer.
6. **Water and sewer connection works themselves are inexpensive, but water supply is unreliable.**
   - Veolia Jur publishes regulated unit prices (Q2-2026, VERIFIED). A 100 m water service line with meter costs **≈ AMD 0.32M**, and a 100 m sewer connection with 2 manholes **≈ AMD 1.2M**, both incl. VAT (ESTIMATE).
   - Supply was rationed to **17 h/day** for selected subscribers in summer 2026 (REPORTED). On-site storage (5–15 m³) is therefore needed.
   - Where there is no public sewer, a packaged biological treatment unit costs roughly **AMD 2–4.5M installed** (ESTIMATE, scaled from Russian list prices). It needs an effluent permit, which I could not verify.
7. **Go all-electric.** I could not obtain Gazprom Armenia connection fees (the site was unreachable and search returned nothing). The non-residential electricity tariff is **53.48 / 43.48 AMD/kWh day/night** incl. VAT, in force since 1 Feb 2022 (VERIFIED). *Fact-check correction: the key-data table dated this tariff "from Feb 2025". Only the single rate of 54.61 AMD/kWh for consumers who fail to obtain qualified-consumer status or who choose another supplier under the wholesale-market rules took effect on 1 Feb 2025. The model should check whether a commercial consumer could fall into that 54.61 rate.* A gas connection adds a separate permit path and timeline for little benefit at this scale.
8. **Ground conditions can turn cheap earthworks into expensive ones.** Veolia's regulated unit rates show machine excavation of ordinary soil at **AMD 730–1,400/m³**, against **AMD 7,260–8,580/m³ for rock (hydraulic hammer)**, i.e. 5–10× (VERIFIED). The volcanic plateau terrain along the M4 makes a **geotechnical survey before land purchase** essential.
9. **US benchmark and ratio.**
   - Dutch Bros spent **$241.1M of capex in FY2025 while opening 141 company-operated shops**, i.e. ≤ $1.71M per opening. That figure is an upper-bound proxy: it includes maintenance and IT capex, and many shops are on build-to-suit or ground leases. Dutch Bros targets lots ≥ 25,000 sq ft (≈ 2,320 m²) (VERIFIED, 10-K).
   - A directly disclosed US drive-thru coffee unit cost is much lower. Black Rock Coffee Bar's IPO prospectus (424B4, 15 Sep 2025) gives a **target net capex of $0.6M per unit**, with new-store development costs historically averaging about $600k blended. Sibling C6 reports **$860k gross / ~$650k net** in 2025 (VERIFIED).
   - The Armenian mid-range build plus coffee and kitchen equipment comes to ≈ **$620k** ($545k from this file plus ≈ $75k equipment from C4/C2). That is **≈ 0.7× a US gross unit cost and ≈ 1.0× a US net unit cost** (ESTIMATE). The flagship (≈ $1.06M before equipment) costs more than a typical US drive-thru coffee unit. **Armenian construction gives no material capex discount against lean US drive-thru coffee formats.**
   - *Fact-check correction: the earlier text said the mid-range build is "≈ 0.3× the US per-shop capex" and "≈ 0.35–0.5×" like-for-like. Those ratios used the Dutch Bros upper-bound proxy (≤ $1.71M, which includes maintenance and IT capex) and have been removed.*
   - I found no Starbucks per-store build cost (not disclosed publicly).
10. **Soft costs typically add 10–14% of hard cost.** Armenia's former public-sector norms (Order 41-N, repealed Dec 2024; VERIFIED) set technical supervision at 2% of the estimate (≤ AMD 50M), author supervision at 0.6% and contingency at 5–6%. Private design fees are not published; I assume 5% (lean) to 10% (flagship) of hard cost. Permit state fees are small (≤ AMD 0.3M for a building up to 500 m²; REPORTED). *Fact-check correction: (a) the hartak.am fees (AMD 75,000 / 150,000) are most likely Yerevan zone-coefficient values; the Law on Local Duties Art. 11 gives AMD 15,000 for a public building ≤ 200 m² and 30,000 × zone coefficient for 200–500 m². (b) Under GD 596-N (sibling C5), a catering building ≤ 300 m² is risk category II. In that category a designer's guarantee letter replaces state design expertise, and a contractor's guarantee replaces technical supervision. Expertise (AMD 0.5–1.5M) and technical supervision (AMD 1.8–4.6M) are therefore optional quality costs, not statutory ones. They stay in the totals but are labelled as optional (≈ AMD 2.3–6.1M per scenario).*

**How I read the index:**
- The 2025 decline coincides with a stronger dram (imported materials are cheaper in AMD).
- The index does not reflect the demand boom. Construction volume grew +20.1% in Jan–Nov 2025 and **+24.7% (to AMD 471.6bn) in Jan–Aug 2026** [5][6]. Housing completions jumped from 855,604 m² (2023) to **1,457,262 m² (2024)** [7].
- Economy-wide labour cost rose +2.7% y/y in Q2 2026 [8]. I found no construction-specific labour index.
- Contractor margins and labour availability are likely to tighten faster than the official index suggests. The Dutch Bros 10-K describes the same dynamic in the US ("delays due to scarcity of construction labor") [26].

**FX.**
- CBA rate on 9 Oct 2026: **361.93 AMD/USD** [1].
- The AMD was about 4.6% stronger y/y in Aug 2026 (index 95.4) [2].
- The 2025 average was 386.82 and the 2026 YTD average 370.81 (ESTIMATE from CBA daily data).
- **Implication:** at 386.8 AMD/USD (the 2025 average) the mid-range scenario would read $510k instead of $545k (fact-check-corrected totals). **Model capex in AMD.** Only imported items (modular kits, drive-thru windows, digital boards, generators, packaged treatment units) should be USD/EUR-linked.

**Escalation assumption for the model (ASSUMPTION): +4–6% p.a. in AMD for 2027 start.** The basis is CPI at 4.4%, PPI at 8.9%, construction volume +25% and the official construction index at +1.5%. I weight towards CPI because labour and margins dominate small projects.

### 3.4 Site development: earthworks, paving, curbs, drainage, lighting, fencing, landscaping

**Primary source.** Veolia Jur's quarterly *"unit prices of works in projects developed under Veolia technical conditions"*, Q2 2026 [14]. ### 3.5 Utility connections

#### 3.5.1 Electricity (ENA, PSRC-regulated)

**Fee formula and rates.**
- The structure is set by PSRC Decision N 659-N (21 Dec 2007, as amended): connection fee = Vst (standard component) + Vlrats (additional component) + Vpahust (reserve-supply line) [11].
- At 0.4 kV the standard component is **Vh (fixed) + Vf (per kVA) × requested kVA** [11].
- Rates changed by PSRC decision on 22 Apr 2026, effective for applications after 27 Apr 2026 [9][10] (REPORTED):

| 0.4 kV, houses and commercial consumers | Before | From 27 Apr 2026 |

| Fixed component (all regions) | 300,000 | **229,000** |
| Per kVA, Yerevan | 46,000 | **16,000** |
| Per kVA, other regions | 20,000 | **9,000** |

- An older ENA guide [11] shows 203,000 fixed + 7,500/kVA × 1.4 (Yerevan) or × 1.0 (regions). Fees therefore rose at some point before 2026 and were then cut in April 2026.

**What the standard fee covers.**
- It covers design, metering, breakers and line construction inside the boundary of a settlement, regardless of distance.
- **Outside a settlement boundary it covers only up to 200 m** from the connection point. Beyond that, the applicant pays the average cost of the extra line and, if the feasibility study requires it, a higher-voltage line and substation, at ENA's published unit costs [11].
- ENA's site was intermittently unavailable (HTTP 503) and I could not locate the unit-cost table. **This is the biggest utility cost unknown for a highway plot.** A new 10/0.4 kV substation plus several hundred metres of 10 kV line could plausibly cost tens of millions of AMD (ASSUMPTION; verify with an ENA technical-conditions request).
- Scenario contingencies: AMD 2M (lean), 6M (mid), 12M (flagship).

**Timeline** [11] (REPORTED):
- Contract within 5 working days of a complete application, then 50% prepayment.
- Maximum connection time from prepayment: 15 working days (0.4 kV simplified, ≤ 30 kVA, no earthworks); **60 days** (other 0.4 kV); **90 days** (6/10 kV). If a higher-voltage line or substation is needed, the higher-voltage deadline applies.
- **Institutional risk:** temporary manager since 18 Jul 2025; licence revoked by PSRC on 17 Nov 2025, with violations of new-connection timelines among the grounds; ENA is disputing it; nationalisation is planned [12].
- **Plan 4–6 months for power at a greenfield highway site** (ASSUMPTION).

**Load sizing (ASSUMPTION, to be confirmed by the MEP engineer):**
- 2–3 group espresso machines, high-speed ovens, refrigeration, a water heater, HVAC (≈ 10–25 kW), lighting/signage and digital boards. That points to **≈ 60 kVA (lean) to 150 kVA (flagship)**.
- All-electric adds water heating and space heating. Inverter heat pumps keep this moderate.

**Tariff and solar.**
- Non-residential 0.38 kV tariff: 53.48 AMD/kWh day, 43.48 night, incl. VAT, in force since 1 Feb 2022 under PSRC 478-N (29 Dec 2021) as amended by 448-N (30 Dec 2024) [13]. ## 4. Implications for the drive-thru concept (critical)

1. **Site selection matters more than building design.**
   - On the M4, the difference between a plot with existing grid capacity, sewer and a permitted access, and a "raw" plot outside a settlement, can exceed the entire lean building budget. Access lanes, substation and treatment unit together come to AMD 20–60M.
   - **Before any land commitment, obtain:** (a) ENA technical conditions for 100–150 kVA, with a written cost estimate of the additional component (Vlrats); (b) Veolia or community technical conditions for water and sewer; (c) the roads authority's access requirements for the M4; (d) a geotechnical report.
2. **The lean route should be "co-located", not "greenfield".** A modular unit on an existing fuel station or retail forecourt with shared access and paving (~$195k all-in) cuts capex by about a quarter and avoids the highest-risk items. That suits a concept that is still unproven in Armenia.
3. **Do not over-pay for "modular".** Armenian site-built steel and sandwich-panel construction is competitive per m². Modular earns its keep only if speed or relocation matters, for example if the plot is leased short-term or demand is uncertain. In that case, a relocatable unit has real option value.
4. **The flagship ($1.0M+) is hard to justify at Armenian revenue levels.**
   - Dutch Bros generates ~$2.1M AUV on ≤ $1.7M capex, about 1.2× sales/capex.
   - An Armenian unit at the same capex would need US-level sales. Even at a 0.5× sales/capex ratio, the flagship needs ≈ $0.5M+ annual sales to make sense.
   - The C/D workstreams (revenue) should test this explicitly. The mid-range ($0.45–0.5M) is the realistic maximum for a first unit.
5. **Timeline risk is material.** The permit itself is quick (≤ 15 working days), but design, expertise, technical conditions and the ENA connection (60–90+ days) make a realistic total **6–10 months from land to opening** for a greenfield site (ASSUMPTION). ENA's licence dispute and nationalisation add uncertainty to connection performance.
6. **Water security is an operating risk for a beverage concept.** The 17 h/day rationing means storage, pumps and treatment must be part of capex, not an afterthought.
7. **Budget in AMD and lock prices.** The official index masks real cost pressure (construction volume +25% y/y). Ask for fixed-price design-and-build contracts with a capped-escalation clause. Order imported items early and price them in EUR/USD.
8. **VAT registration is worth ~1/6 of capex.** If the operating company is VAT-registered at the time of construction, input VAT on capex (~AMD 15–63M across scenarios, i.e. 1/6 of the gross totals) is recoverable. The tax workstream should confirm the thresholds and timing.
