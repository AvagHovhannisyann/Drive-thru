## 1. Executive summary

1. **A highway drive-thru on the M4 is legally possible, but it sits inside three overlapping "special regulation" regimes.** These regimes, not the building permit, drive the timeline.
   - **Regime 1, interstate road zone:** 70 m from the road axis outside settlements. Inside settlements it is the first building line, and at least 50 m from the red line. Source: Law on Highways Art. 13; Government Decision (GD) 2404-N.
   - **Regime 2, Sevan basin:** the Lake Sevan "immediate impact zone" covers the whole catchment, and the Sevan National Park protection zone covers the same area. Sources: Law on Lake Sevan Art. 8; GD 927-N §12–13.
   - **Regime 3, community zoning:** master plan, or a land-designation change (Land Code; GD 1918-N).
   - Inside the road zone, any building, any access road or entrance, and any non-traffic sign needs the **road owner's agreement**. The road owner is the state road body, i.e. the Ministry of Territorial Administration and Infrastructure (MTAI). (VERIFIED.)
2. **The building itself qualifies for the simplified track.** A catering building of **≤300 m², with at most 2 floors above ground and 1 below**, is **risk category II** (GD 596-N, Annex 4, List 2, §3(1)(գ)). Fuel stations are excluded from this category.
   - **State design expertise is replaced by the designer's guarantee letter.**
   - The Architectural-Planning Assignment (APA, ՃՀԱ) is issued in **10 days**.
   - Design approval and the construction permit are issued together in **5 days**.
   - Fees are small:
     - the APA is free;
     - the construction-permit local duty is **AMD 15,000** for a public building of ≤200 m² (VERIFIED, Law on Local Duties Art. 11). That is far below the AMD 75,000 quoted on hartak.am, which I read as the Yerevan figure (see §3.3).
3. **Highway access is the critical path, and the Armenian norm now sets it concretely** (ՀՀՇՆ 32-01-2022, in force 2022, amended 2025; VERIFIED).
   - Any connection to an IA–IC road carrying **≥50 vehicles/day** in or out must have **deceleration and acceleration lanes**:
     - **acceleration lane: 140–230 m at full width plus an 80 m taper;**
     - **deceleration lane: 90–110 m at full width plus an 80 m taper**;
     - both as wide as the adjacent lane, separated by a 0.75 m strip.
   - On an expressway (category IB), at-grade access is allowed only **without crossing the through road**, i.e. right-in / right-out only, with **no left turns or median crossings**.
   - Speed-change lanes total about **1,155–1,500 m² of heavy-duty pavement, roughly AMD 27–43M** (ESTIMATE). That is more than C1's mid-range allowance of 1,125 m² / AMD 28M, and well above its lean allowance of 700 m² / AMD 17.8M.
   - **No statutory deadline or fee schedule was found for the road owner's connection approval.** Approval is discretionary, needs the Police's agreement on the traffic scheme, and the developer pays for all traffic-control equipment (Law on Highways Art. 13(16)(ա), 15.3).
4. **An environmental impact assessment (EIA) may be triggered by the paving, not the building. This is the most under-appreciated risk.**
   - The 2014 EIA Law, as consolidated to 18 Jul 2026, puts **"landscaping/improvement works with ≥1,000 m² of impervious surface"** in **Category B** (Art. 12(4)(8)(գ); VERIFIED).
   - A drive-thru pad with stacking lanes, bypass, parking and access roads can exceed 1,000 m² of asphalt (ESTIMATE, §3.7).
   - Category B adds three steps:
     - a community public hearing, then a **council consent vote that can veto the project**;
     - state expertise of up to **40 working days (+30)**;
     - a duty of **AMD 315,000**.
   - The whole EIA sequence takes **about 5–6.5 months** (ESTIMATE).
   - Permeable paving for parking is a design lever to keep under the threshold. Whether the threshold applies at all must be confirmed with the Ministry of Environment.
5. **Changing the land designation is a 2–4-month interagency process, and it carries a cash cost** (ASSUMPTION for the realistic duration).
   - In a community without an approved master plan, the change goes through GD 1918-N: community → marzpet → regional commission → MTAI → interagency commission. **Every member must agree; otherwise the case goes to a Government committee.** The council then votes.
   - Statutory steps total about **28 working days plus a council session** (VERIFIED for the steps).
   - The owner pays the community the **difference in cadastral value** (Land Code Art. 7(8); VERIFIED). The amount is site-specific and was not found.
6. **The Sevan end of the corridor is heavily restricted** (GD 1563-N; VERIFIED).
   - **Subzone 1:** shore up to **1,905.0 m** above sea level, inside the national park. Main buildings are prohibited; only removable "non-main" structures on rights of up to 3 years.
   - **Subzone 2:** the rest of the park. Building rights on state or community land go through an **interagency commission**, for up to 25 years.
   - **Subzone 3:** a **500 m strip outside the park**. Normal procedure applies, but the strip is a special-regulation area.
   - **Anywhere in the Sevan catchment**, economic activity must be agreed with the authorised environment body, and a **pre-activity declaration** is decided within **one month** (Law on Lake Sevan Art. 25; NP Charter §13).
   - **Practical reading:** keep the first unit west of Sevan town (Abovyan–Hrazdan) unless a site has clear zoning.
7. **Food-safety compliance is mature and enforced. One change matters for the opening date: from 1 Jan 2027 an approved HACCP plan becomes a registration document** (Law HO-98-N Art. 4; VERIFIED).
   - HACCP-based procedures are already mandatory for every catering class. The last deadline, for micro-enterprises, was 1 Jan 2023 (GD 827-N; VERIFIED).
   - Operators must register with the FSIB **before starting activity**. From 2027 the registration must include the HACCP procedures and the approved plan.
   - Inspections use a weighted catering checklist (GD 1266-N, Annex 3). Frequency is capped at **once a year for high-risk operators, once every 3 years for medium and once every 5 years for low** (Law on Inspections Art. 4; VERIFIED).
   - The FSIB suspended a catering outlet on 5 Oct 2026 for non-washable walls and **no staff changing room** (VERIFIED).
8. **Staff and operating rules that touch the business model** (VERIFIED):
   - **Medical books:** catering staff are examined **on hiring and then every six months** (GD 347-N). Staff without a valid book are barred from work by the FSIB.
   - **Cook qualifications:** cooks and pastry chefs need a **vocational diploma or certificate, or a certificate of at least 1 year of work-based learning** (Law on Trade and Services Art. 12(6)).
   - **Menus** must show the **portion mass and the final price** (Art. 12(3)). This applies to drive-thru menu boards.
   - **Name badges** with a photo are compulsory (Art. 12(4)).
   - **Sanitary units** (toilets) are compulsory at every catering outlet (Art. 11(4)). On the interstate road zone the APA can also require **public toilets** (GD 2404-N §5(է)).
9. **Branding: a foreign-language name is fine only as a registered trademark, and Armenian text must lead.**
   - All signboards and advertising must be in Armenian. Other languages may be added (Law on Language Art. 4(գ)).
   - Advertising in a foreign language **only** is banned, **except registered trademarks**. Any foreign text must not exceed the Armenian text in area or visual weight, and the Armenian must come **first or above** (Law on Advertising Art. 5).
   - **File the trademark in Armenia before signage is fabricated.** Fees are about AMD 120–150k nationally (REPORTED, C4).
10. **Roadside advertising outside settlements needs an MTAI permit, which is agreed with the Police** (GD 765-N; VERIFIED).
    - The decision comes within **30 days**; the permit lasts **5 years**, extendable a year at a time; the sign must be installed **within 6 months**.
    - Design limits:
      - **≤27 m² per board**;
      - lower edge **≥2.2 m** above the road surface;
      - side edge **≥3.5 m** from the asphalt;
      - **≥150 m** from junctions, bus stops and bridges;
      - spacing of **200 m** between boards of 15–27 m² on roads above 60 km/h;
      - refused where visibility is **under 350 m**.
    - Boards also pay **road tax of AMD 10,000 per m² per year** (REPORTED, C4, Tax Code Art. 190).
11. **Realistic time from securing land to opening** (ESTIMATE, §3.10):
    - **8–11 months** for an in-settlement or brownfield site with the correct designation and existing access;
    - **12–20 months** for a greenfield M4 roadside site needing a designation change, new access lanes and possibly an EIA;
    - **18–30 months, or infeasible**, inside the Sevan park zones.
    - C1's "6–10 months" is optimistic for the greenfield case.

### 3.2 Food safety

**Legal frame (VERIFIED).**
- Law HO-135-N "On Food Safety" (2014, consolidated to 15 Jul 2026) [10] sets operator duties.
- Law HO-143-N "On State Control of Food Safety" (2014) [12] sets FSIB control, including HACCP audits.
- The EAEU technical regulation TR CU 021/2011 applies directly. The FSIB's own HACCP page cites its Art. 10 as the basis for HACCP-based procedures [32].

**HACCP.**
- Food Safety Law Art. 8(7) requires a "good hygienic and production practice and HACCP self-control system". Art. 10 lists the seven HACCP principles and applies them to production, processing and distribution after primary production [10].
- GD 827-N of 23 Jul 2015 sets the deadlines [13]:
  - **restaurants (56.10.1), fast food (56.10.2), catering for organisations (56.29.x) and bars (56.30)** fall in group 4;
  - deadlines were 1 Jan 2020 (large), 2021 (medium), 2022 (small) and **1 Jan 2023 (micro)**;
  - bakery production (10.71) and coffee and tea processing (10.83) had earlier or equal deadlines;
  - **result: HACCP is now mandatory for any new operator from day one.**
- Law HO-98-N of 18 Apr 2025 [11] makes two changes:
  - it lets the policy ministry approve HACCP implementation guidelines (Art. 8(8.1));
  - **from 1 Jan 2027**, catering and food-production operators must file "information on HACCP-based procedures and the approved plan" **as part of FSIB registration** (Art. 17(1)(6)).
- The FSIB publishes guides, including "HACCP-based procedures for catering facilities" and an SME technical guide [32].
- **Implication:** the HACCP plan has to exist before opening. Budget a consultant or in-house food-safety lead in pre-opening.

**Registration of food business operators (VERIFIED).**
- Food Safety Law Art. 17 [10]: operators register with the registry body **before actual activity starts**. Information required: name, activity type, location, tax ID and registration number (plus the HACCP plan from 2027).
- Changes must be notified within **3 days**.
- Registration is online on snund.am [33]. **No fee is set in the law.**
- The catering checklist itself asks whether the operator is in the database (item 1.21) [14].

**Hygiene and premises requirements.**
- The 2003 Ministry of Health catering sanitary rules (SanPiN N 2-III-4.2-01-2003) were **repealed on 24 May 2022** by MoH Order 31-N [15] (VERIFIED). Older consultants' checklists citing them are out of date.
- Current requirements come from:
  - the Food Safety Law, Art. 13–16;
  - TR CU 021/2011;
  - GD N 34-N of 20 Jan 2011, which the FSIB checklist cites as its legal basis (not opened).
- The FSIB catering checklist (**GD 1266-N of 11 Aug 2022, Annex 3, NACE 56**, consolidated to 16 Aug 2025) [14] checks premises items that hit the design brief directly:

| Checklist item | Requirement (paraphrased) | Design implication |
|---|---|---|
| 1.4 | Enough ventilated sanitary units **connected to a working sewer**; doors must not open directly into food rooms | **On a non-sewered highway plot, a septic or treatment-unit solution may be challenged; confirm with FSIB** (C1 §3 already flags discharge permits) |
| 1.5–1.6 | Hand-wash basins with **hot and cold water**, soap, disinfectant and hygienic drying, located **separately** from food-washing sinks | Separate hand-wash sinks at the window station and the prep line |
| 1.7–1.8 | Natural plus mechanical ventilation; adequate lighting | Kitchen extract and makeup air |
| 1.18 | Food waste in closed, cleanable containers | Bin store |
| 1.20 | **Uninterrupted drinking-water supply** | A borehole needs a water-use permit and lab testing (Water Code) |
| (enforcement) | Smooth, washable, impervious walls, floors and surfaces; a **staff changing room** | FSIB suspended an outlet in Stepanavan on 5 Oct 2026 for exactly these points [35] |

- The Law on Trade and Services Art. 11(4) [17] requires **every catering outlet** to have access and pedestrian paths, information signs and **sanitary units**.
- GD 2404-N §5(է) [3] lets the APA require **public toilets** at service objects along interstate and republican roads. Where there is no sewer, treatment must be provided.

**Inspection regime (VERIFIED).**
- Inspections are risk-based (Law on Inspections Art. 3) [30]:
  - **high risk: at most once a year;**
  - **medium: at most once every 3 years;**
  - **low: at most once every 5 years** (Art. 4(3)).
- Unscheduled inspections can follow complaints.
- The FSIB publishes annual inspection programmes and results on snund.am.
- Checklist questions carry weights of 0.3 to 1 point [14].
- **I did not find which risk class a new drive-thru would fall into** (§5).

**Staff health (VERIFIED).**
- GD 347-N of 27 Mar 2003, consolidated to 15 Nov 2025 [16], Annex 2 row 1: catering and food-trade staff need examinations **on hiring and then every six months**, recorded in a **personal sanitary (medical) book** on the official form.
- The FSIB head can bar from work anyone without a valid book (§22).
- **Cost per examination was not found** (§5).

**Other operating rules (VERIFIED, Law on Trade and Services [17]).**
- Art. 12(3): the menu must show **dish mass and the final payable price**. Drive-thru menu boards must therefore show grams or ml.
- Art. 12(4): photo name badges.
- Art. 12(5): uniforms.
- Art. 12(6): **cooks and pastry chefs need vocational qualification evidence**.
- Art. 11(7): a signboard meeting Government requirements (the implementing decision was not located).
- Food Safety Law Art. 9(2): **pre-packed** grab-and-go items must be labelled in Armenian, at least 3 mm type, listing composition, allergens, mass, dates and so on [10].

### 3.3 Construction permitting

**Statute.**
- The Law on Urban Development (HO-217, 1998, consolidated) [6]:
  - Art. 23: the construction permit is issued by the **community head through the online permit system**, after the APA authority approves the design.
  - Art. 25: the final act and operating permit; **operating before the operating permit is prohibited**.
- GD 596-N of 19 Mar 2015 [7] is the master procedure. It was **amended repeatedly in 2025–2026, most recently with changes in force from 9 Oct 2026**. The category III list (List 2.1) was repealed by GD 535-N of 18 Apr 2026, so category III now operates by default definition (§27(3)). **Expect churn; re-check the consolidated text when the application is filed.**

**Risk categories (GD 596-N §26–27; Annex 4) (VERIFIED).**

| Category | What qualifies (relevant extracts) | Expertise / quality control |
|---|---|---|
| I (low) | Current repairs, interior fit-out, landscaping and similar minor works | No APA or permit needed (List 1) |

### 3.4 Land designation, master plans and zoning

- **Land categories** (Land Code Art. 6) [8] include settlement lands; industrial; energy, transport and utility lands; specially protected areas; and others. Each plot also has a **functional designation**, recorded in the cadastre and rights certificates (Art. 7(4)–(5)).
- **Unauthorised change of use** is grounds for annulling transactions and refusing registration (Art. 7(7)).
- **Cost of a change (VERIFIED):** when the owner initiates a change, it pays the **community budget the difference in cadastral value** if the new value is higher (Art. 7(8)). Some agricultural-processing uses are exempt; catering is not on that list.
- **Communities with an approved master plan:** the designation must follow the plan. A use contrary to the plan needs a plan amendment, which is a months-long process (ASSUMPTION; GD 73615-series procedures exist but were not opened).
- **Communities without an approved master plan** (Urban Development Law Art. 30(2); GD 1918-N) [6][9], VERIFIED steps:
  1. The owner applies to the community head with a land-management file. It covers the cadastral code, current and proposed designation, investment and jobs, a Land Code Art. 60 certificate, the community head's opinion, and a plan showing sanitary-protection zones, **state roads within 70 m**, utility safety zones and monument zones.
  2. Community head → marzpet in **5 working days**; marzpet → regional commission in **2**; commission opinions in **3** (silence = positive); marzpet conclusion → MTAI in **2**.
  3. MTAI → interagency commission in **3**; members respond in **5** (silence = positive). A positive conclusion needs **all members**. If at least two-thirds agree, the case goes to the **Government's Committee on Territorial Development and Environment** at its next session.
  4. MTAI summarises in **3** and returns the file through the marzpet. The community head puts the decision to the council's **next session** within **5** working days.
  5. **Statutory sum about 28 working days plus the council session; realistic 2–4 months** (ESTIMATE/ASSUMPTION). Rejection grounds include norms, third-party rights and safety [9] §16.
- **Interstate road overlay:** in the road protection zone and settlement transit sections, designation changes follow the law, and **alienation of community land** must first be agreed with the state road body and the traffic-safety body (Law on Highways Art. 14(5)) [1].
- **Master-plan status** of Abovyan, Charentsavan, Hrazdan, Sevan and the villages along the M4 **was not found** on ARLIS (master plans are council acts). Verify with each community and the Urban Development Committee registry (§5).
- **Buying or leasing state or community land:** normally by auction or tender with a draft APA attached (GD 596-N §42–44) [7]. In Sevan park subzones 1–2, the **interagency commission** acts as the tender commission [24].

### 3.5 Highway access (Law on Highways, GD 2404-N, ՀՀՇՆ 32-01-2022)

**Zones and prohibitions (VERIFIED, Law HO-240-N of 5 Dec 2006, consolidated with HO-299-N of 12 Jun 2024) [1].**
- **Right-of-way** (օտարման շերտ): the road body plus 1 m on each side, and at junctions the area along the outer edge of right-turn arms (Art. 2). It is managed by the state road body (Art. 13(4)).
- **Protection zone** (պաշտպանական գոտի), outside settlements only: **70 m from axis for interstate roads**, 50 m republican, 30 m regional (Art. 13(2)). The land remains the owner's (Art. 13(5)).
- **Prohibited without agreement** of the road manager and other interested bodies, in the protection zone and in transit sections through settlements (Art. 13(6)):
  - building or rebuilding buildings, including utility lines, and surveys;
  - non-traffic signs, and planting or lighting that obstructs visibility;
  - **building connecting roads, entries and exits**.
- **Landowners in the protection zone must** (Art. 13(16)):
  - build any connecting road **only after agreeing it** with the road manager and the traffic-safety authority;
  - keep waste and materials **≥10 m** from the road edge;
  - prevent vehicles entering the road at undesignated points;
  - remove structures on the road body's demand for repairs.
- **Traffic-management equipment** needed because of a development is installed **at the developer's cost**, and traffic-organisation schemes must be agreed in advance with the state road body and the Government-authorised traffic-safety body (Art. 15.3).
- **Service objects** (fuel, repair, trade and "other service objects") may be placed in the right-of-way and protection zone **with the road manager's permission** and in line with building norms (Art. 14(1)–(2)). The **community head submits the site plan** relative to the road to the state road body (Art. 14(4)).
- **Government list of permitted objects** in protection zones of interstate and republican roads (Art. 13(15.2), added 2024): **not found**. It may not yet be adopted (§5).
- **Selling goods** on the carriageway, median or shoulders is prohibited, and so is leaving or entering the road at undesignated places (Art. 10(6)(դ),(ե)). **Drive-thru queues must never spill onto the shoulder.**
- **Road-safety audit** (Art. 19.2): mandatory at all stages of road-construction programmes, by a certified auditor independent of the designer and contractor. In force for **interstate roads from 1 Jan 2026** (Art. 21(4)). Whether this applies to a private junction is unclear (§5).

**GD 2404-N of 29 Dec 2005, amended 2022 (VERIFIED) [3]: interstate and republican roads plus adjacent land are a "special-regulation object" (Urban Development Law Art. 19(5)(6)).**
- Width: **70 m from axis outside settlements**. Inside settlements: first-line plots, at least 50 m from the red line (§2).
- **Side visibility setback from the carriageway edge: 18.0 m** on interstate roads outside settlements (§4).
- The APA must require (§5):
  - access **from secondary or auxiliary roads, minimising junctions** with the interstate road;
  - parking outside the traffic zone;
  - grade-separated pedestrian crossings where crowds gather;
  - noise screens where noise limits are exceeded;
  - utilities outside the road body;
  - **public toilets at service objects**, with treatment if there is no sewer;
  - landscaping;
  - **accessibility for disabled people**.
- Outdoor trade in the first line is allowed only in designated areas that keep parking off the road (§6).
- Land-use proposals and development are **coordinated with the Urban Development Committee through the marzpetaran** (§8), with the timelines in GD 792-N (§3.3).

### 3.6 Outdoor advertising, signage, language and trademarks

**Roadside boards on state roads (GD 765-N of 19 Jan 2006, amended 30 Sep 2021 and 26 Feb 2026) (VERIFIED) [2].**
- **Scope:** boards in the right-of-way and protection zones of state roads outside settlement transit sections. This **expressly includes boards informing road users about service objects such as snack bars** (§3).
- **Permit-issuer:** MTAI for interstate and republican roads; the marzpet for regional roads; the community head inside settlement transit sections (§4, 4.1). The Police must agree in all cases.
- **Application** (electronic): application form, board design or sketch, two site photos, and from 2026 **the landowner's written consent** for boards in protection zones (§5).
- **Timing:**
  - decision within **30 days**, with inspection and Police agreement (§6);
  - technical conditions are issued with the permit (§7);
  - compliance act within **7 days** of the completion notice (§8);
  - permit valid **5 years** from the compliance act, extendable by one year at a time, with the request filed ≥30 days before expiry (§8.1–8.2);
  - the permit lapses if the board is not installed within 6 months, or if the compliance act is not requested within 3 months of installation (§8.4).
- **Refusal grounds** (§10(գ)): siting on the road body or median, on accident-prone sections, at rail crossings, tunnels or bridges, on retaining walls, trees or rocks, on steep grades or sharp curves, **or where visibility is under 350 m**.
- **Geometry** (§14–15): see table row 33.
- **Upkeep** (§18, rewritten 2026): technical inspection every 6 months; repaint yearly; repair crash damage within 15 days.
- **Road tax:** AMD 10,000 per m² per year for boards on state roads outside settlement transit sections (Law on Highways Art. 14(3); Tax Code Art. 190; REPORTED via C4). Example (ESTIMATE): a two-faced 3 × 6 m pylon = 36 m² → **AMD 360,000/yr (USD 995)**.
- **Is an on-site pylon "advertising" or a "signboard" (ցուցանակ)?**
  - The Law on Language defines a signboard as a public inscription **placed at the organisation's premises** with its name or trademark (Art. 1.1) [21].
  - A fascia sign is therefore a signboard.
  - **A tall pylon in the protection zone, visible from the highway, is likely to be treated as advertising** by MTAI (needing a permit and road tax) and is in any case caught by Law on Highways Art. 13(6)(դ) (ASSUMPTION; §5).

**In settlements.**
- Outdoor advertising follows council rules. A community-head permit is required (Law on Advertising Art. 11) [20].
- Local duty for "other outdoor advertising" is **AMD 1,500 per m² per month**; a company's own advertising on its own carrier pays 10% of that (Law on Local Duties Art. 11(1)(14)) [18].

**Language (VERIFIED).**

### 3.7 Environmental: Lake Sevan, the national park and EIA

**Law on Lake Sevan, HO-190 of 15 May 2001, consolidated 14 Dec 2024 (VERIFIED) [22].**
- **Ecological zoning** (Art. 6–9):
  - **central zone** = Sevan National Park;
  - **immediate impact zone** = the **whole catchment outside the park, up to the watershed**;
  - **indirect impact zone** = the rest of Armenia that may affect the lake.
- Both the central and immediate zones are **special-regulation objects of urban development** (Art. 7(2), 8(2)). See also Urban Development Law Art. 19(5)(2).
- In the immediate zone, technologies whose emissions or wastewater exceed norms are prohibited, among other bans (Art. 10(2)).
- **Art. 25:** economic operators must submit a **declaration to the authorised body before starting activity**; the body decides within **one month**, based on expertise.
- Art. 23: land allocation in the central and immediate zones follows **landscape and urban-planning plans**.

**Sevan National Park.**
- **GD 927-N (2002) Charter** [25]:
  - the park's **protection zone is the entire Sevan catchment** (§12);
  - inside it, "any economic activity that does not harm" the park's ecosystems is permitted **if agreed with the authorised state body** (§13).
  - Park zones: reserve, sanctuary, **recreational** and **economic** (§7). In the recreational and economic zones, land **above 1,905.0 m** may be leased or given building rights in line with zoning projects (§24, 30), with **positive environmental and urban-development expertise** (§25).
- **GD 1563-N of 18 Dec 2008, amended 2021** [24], sets the subzones:
  - **Subzone 1:** from the water line up to **1,905.0 m**, the area to be flooded as the lake rises. **No main buildings.** Only non-main structures; building rights ≤3 years.
  - **Subzone 2:** the rest of the park. Main buildings are possible. State or community land is allocated through the **interagency commission**, for up to 25 years (extendable to 2060 if investment is ≥AMD 150M). Contracts are signed with the Sevan NP SNCO.
  - **Subzone 3:** a **500 m strip** outside the park boundary, covering parts of the communities in Annex 1 (the list was not readable in the ARLIS text). Normal procedure with **no extra term limits**, but APAs must follow zoning projects. Until those are approved, draft APAs need **Urban Development Committee agreement through the Gegharkunik marzpet** (§4(3)).
- The Sevan programme law (HO-276 of 14 Dec 2001, consolidated 13 Sep 2025) [23] aims to **raise the lake by at least 6 m**. It recorded the level at 1,896.32 m when adopted and 1,900.46 m on 1 Jan 2017. This explains the 1,905 m line.

**EIA (Law HO-110-N of 21 Jun 2014, consolidated 18 Jul 2026) (VERIFIED) [26].**
- Activities are split into Categories A and B (Art. 12(2)).
- **Category B, urban development** (Art. 12(4)(8)):
  - (ա) buildings with **≥1,500 m²** largest above-ground footprint, or ≥2,000 m² underground;
  - (բ) **≥15,000 m²** total building area **including impervious landscaped area**;
  - (գ) **landscaping or improvement with ≥1,000 m² impervious surface**.
- **Category B, energy and transport:** fuel stations with **≥5 m³** storage (relevant to a fuel-station tie-up).
- Art. 12(6): Government-listed activities in **specially protected areas, forest lands and settlement green belts** also need Category B expertise.
- **Procedure for Category B:**
  - the initiator notifies the community head (Art. 16);
  - the community publishes notice within 5 working days and **holds a hearing on working day 21–25**;
  - **the council votes on preliminary consent within 30 working days**; if several communities are involved, **one community's refusal counts as refusal** (Art. 16(5));
  - expertise takes **up to 40 working days**, extendable by 30, with a **second hearing** (Art. 13(15)–(16), 27);
  - if the expertise body misses its deadline, the conclusion is deemed positive (Art. 13(17)).
- **State duty:** 300 × base duty (AMD 1,000) = **AMD 300,000**, plus 15× (AMD 15,000) for application review (Law on State Duty, §12.4) [27]. **Total AMD 315,000 (USD 870).**

  - internal entry and exit roads, 300–600 m².

## 4. Implications for the drive-thru concept (critical)

1. **Prefer sites at existing interchanges or inside settlement limits on secondary roads.**
   - GD 2404-N §5(ա) explicitly steers service access to secondary roads.
   - Such sites avoid M4 speed-change lanes worth AMD 27–43M, avoid MTAI connection discretion, and can capture both directions (B1).
   - A "pure" roadside plot on the dual carriageway is the **most expensive and slowest** option, and serves one direction only.
2. **Keep the building ≤300 m², single storey, without fuel retail.** That locks in category II: guarantee letter instead of expertise, a 10-day APA and a 5-day permit.
   - A co-location with a fuel station needs early written classification from the community or the Urban Development Committee. Fuel stations are category IV and EIA Category B if storage is ≥5 m³.
3. **Design the pad to stay below 1,000 m² of impervious surface where possible.** Use permeable pavers for parking, and minimise drive-aisle widths consistent with stacking.
   - Get a Ministry of Environment opinion on Art. 12(4)(8)(գ) **before buying land**. A council veto at the EIA consent stage is a real local-politics risk.
4. **Do not underwrite any site east of Hrazdan without a GIS check of the Sevan zones.**
   - Subzone 1 (below 1,905 m in the park) bans main buildings.
   - The whole catchment needs environment-body agreement and a declaration.
   - Sevan is attractive for summer leisure flows (B1) but is a **phase-2** location at best.
5. **Budget and schedule.**
   - Raise C1's access-lane line to about 1,155–1,500 m² (AMD 27–43M) for any roadside scenario.
   - Add a contingency of **6–9 months** to the greenfield timeline for designation, EIA and access.
   - Model the opening date as Scenario B (12–20 months) unless a Scenario A site is secured.
6. **Make HACCP and FSIB readiness a pre-opening workstream.** From 2027 the HACCP plan is a **registration document**.
   - The design must include: separate hand-wash sinks with hot water, a staff changing room, washable finishes, sanitary units connected to a sewer or an approved treatment solution, and an uninterrupted potable water supply.
7. **The brand must lead with Armenian.**
   - The English name needs **trademark registration in Armenia** before launch.
   - All signage and menu boards must put Armenian first or above, with foreign text no larger or brighter.
   - Menu boards must show **portion mass and final price**.
   - Packaged grab-and-go items need Armenian labels (≥3 mm).
   - The brand workstream should check its signage textures against Law on Advertising Art. 5.
8. **Staffing.**
   - Medical books every six months add recurring cost and scheduling friction.
   - Cooks need documented vocational training, which limits the "train-from-zero" hiring model for kitchen roles. Baristas and cashiers are not named in Art. 12(6).
9. **Precedent cuts both ways.** Coffee House already operates an M4 drive-thru at Ptghni (A2), so the format is permittable. Studying its access geometry and permit trail (cadastre and community records) is the fastest way to learn what MTAI accepts.
10. **The biggest legal uncertainties are procedural, not prohibitive.** No national law bans drive-thrus or late-night catering. The risks are time, cost and discretionary approvals by MTAI, the Police, the Urban Development Committee and the community council. Investors should see the permitting plan as a **gated process**, with land purchase conditional on access and zoning comfort letters.
